# Two complete metro lines already fit Bombay Bawaal

In mid-November 2026 Mumbai runs **six metro services on roughly 91–101 km of track**, and the two that matter most for the game sit entirely inside its current map box: the elevated **Line 1** (Versova–Andheri–Ghatkopar, 12 stations, 11.2 km) and the fully underground **Line 3** (Cuffe Parade–Aarey JVLR, 27 stations, 33 km), together with the **17-station Monorail**, which is built and safety-certified but has carried **no passengers since 20 September 2025**. The other services, Line 2A (Andheri West–Dahisar East), Line 7 (Gundavali–Dahisar East, now running through onto the first four stations of Line 9 to Kashigaon) and the six-station Chembur–Mandale stub of Line 2B, only touch the map's northern and eastern edges; Lines 4, 5, 6, 7A, 8, 10–14 are under construction or on paper. Trains run from about **5:30 am to 11:00–11:50 pm**, every **3 min 20 s on Line 1, 4 min 25 s on Line 3** and up to 9.5 min on Line 2B at peak, and fares climb in **₹10 steps by distance, from ₹10 to ₹60** end to end on Line 3, sold mostly as QR (Quick Response) code tickets. Crowding is lopsided: Line 1 moves about **5.5 lakh (550,000) riders a weekday in four-coach trains** and runs near crush between Ghatkopar and Andheri, while the eight-coach Line 3 carries about **2.25 lakh** and stays roomy, with under a third as many riders per coach. For the game this is unusually good news, because the project's own OpenStreetMap extract already yields both direction centrelines of every operating line in game coordinates, with stop distances, Marathi names, 82 entrances and 119 platform outlines ([osm-metro.json](../osm-metro.json)). The remaining work is modelling viaducts and stations, a timetable that respects the game's 60× clock, and renaming: sponsor-branded station names, operator and app brands, and a handful of stations named after companies must change before anything reaches the screen. The genuine unknowns are official hourly ridership, weekend timetables, exact train and station dimensions, and announcement wording, none of which surfaced in reachable sources.

## Six services run on about 91 km while the monorail waits

Three organisations run Mumbai's metro, and their split explains much of what a rider experiences. **Line 1** belongs to Mumbai Metro One Private Limited (MMOPL), **74 % owned by Reliance Infrastructure and 26 % by the Mumbai Metropolitan Region Development Authority (MMRDA)** ([Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/news/reliance-infrastructure-wins-157-64-crore-arbitration-award-from-mmrda-in-mumbai-metro-dispute-246-PE1_CORP)). The state's plan to buy Reliance's stake for about ₹4,000 crore (1 crore = 10 million) was dropped in favour of a one-time debt settlement ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/maharashtra-cabinet-asks-mmrda-to-pursue-one-time-settlement-of-mumbai-metro-debt-with-lenders)); on **9 July 2026** MMOPL signed a restructuring of ₹2,771.32 crore of debt with the National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited (NARCL) ([Social News XYZ](https://www.socialnews.xyz/2026/07/10/reliance-infras-mumbai-metro-one-signs-debt-restructuring-pact-with-narcl)), and on 27 July won a ₹157.64 crore arbitration award against MMRDA ([Business Upturn](https://businessupturn.com/business/reliance-infrastructure-awarded-rs-157-64-crore-in-mmrda-arbitration-case/)). Lines **2A, 7, 9 and 2B** are owned by MMRDA and run by the Maha Mumbai Metro Operation Corporation Limited (MMMOCL) ([MMMOCL](https://www.mmmocl.co.in/project_brief)), which also runs the Monorail and issues the "Mumbai 1" travel card ([MMMOCL FAQ](https://www.mmmocl.co.in/faq)). **Line 3** is built and run by the Mumbai Metro Rail Corporation (MMRC) ([OpenStreetMap relation 17876723](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/17876723); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-line-3-boosts-train-frequency-with-46-additional-weekday-trips-amid-rising-passenger-demand)). Because each operator sells its own tickets and cards, there is still no single metro card across the city.

### Line 1 climbs over three busy roads from the sea to Ghatkopar

The OpenStreetMap stops span 10.90 km, a little shorter than the official 11.4 km because the official figure includes track beyond the platforms.

### Lines 2A, 7 and 9 meet at Dahisar East, far north of the map

Line 2A runs north from **Andheri (West)**, which sits beside Line 1's D N Nagar, through Lower Oshiwara, Oshiwara, Goregaon (West), Bangur Nagar, Lower Malad, Malad (West), Valnai–Meeth Chowky, Dahanukarwadi, Kandivali (West), Shimpoli, Borivali (West), Eksar, Mandapeshwar–I.C. Colony, Kandarpada and Anand Nagar to **Dahisar (East)**, 17 stations on 18.6 km above the Link Road corridor ([MMRDA](https://mmrda.maharashtra.gov.in/en/projects/transport/metro-line-2A/overview)). Three of these names (Shimpoli, Valnai–Meeth Chowky, Bangur Nagar) were changed in April 2023 after commuter complaints ([Knocksense](https://www.knocksense.com/mumbai/following-frequent-complains-mmrda-renames-three-stations-on-metro-lines-2a-and-7)). Line 7 runs north from **Gundavali**, linked to Line 1's Western Express Highway station, through Mogra, Jogeshwari (East), Goregaon (East), Aarey, Dindoshi, Kurar, Akurli, Poisar, Magathane, Devipada, Rashtriya Udyan and Ovaripada to **Dahisar (East)**, 14 stations above the Western Express Highway ([ixigo](https://www.ixigo.com/metro/mumbai/route-map)). At Dahisar (East) riders change between the red and yellow lines **at concourse level without leaving the paid area** ([Dahisar East station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahisar_East_metro_station)).

Since Line 9 opened on **8 April 2026**, Lines 2A and 7 run as separate corridors and **Line 7 trains run straight through onto Line 9**: Dahisar (East), Pandurang Wadi, Miragaon, Kashigaon, giving a direct Gundavali–Kashigaon ride ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/good-news-commuters-now-travel-from-andheri-to-kashigaon-at-rs-50-know-fares-on-newly-launched-mumbai-metro-line-9)). Phase 2 adds Sai Baba Nagar, Medetiya Nagar, Shaheed Bhagat Singh Garden and Subhash Chandra Bose Stadium in Bhayandar, with civil works 96 % done and a December 2026 target that still needs the Commissioner of Metro Rail Safety (CMRS) ([Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/news-brief/four-new-stations-on-mumbai-metro-line-9-phase-2-expected-to-open-by-december-after-safety-inspection)). Published phase-1 lengths disagree (4.7 km from MMRDA, 5.6 km from ThePrint, 3.57 km between the end stops in OpenStreetMap). Only three of these 35 stations lie inside the game's map: Andheri (West) on 2A (1.2 km of line), and Mogra and Gundavali on Line 7 (2.9 km). Lower Oshiwara and Jogeshwari (East) sit just north of the box edge at latitude 19.140.

### Line 2B is an isolated stub at Chembur, its middle still unbuilt

The open part of Line 2B runs **Chembur → Diamond Garden → Shivaji Chowk (Chembur) → Deonar (formerly "BSNL") → Mankhurd → Mandale**, 5.5 km on a viaduct ending at the Mandale depot ([MMRDA](https://mmrda.maharashtra.gov.in/en/projects/transport/metro-line-2b/overview)). Mankhurd connects to the Harbour line, and the Chembur station, opened 13 August 2026, sits about **200 m from Chembur railway station and 50–100 m from the Monorail's V N Purav Marg station** ([Time Out Mumbai](https://www.timeout.com/mumbai/news/mumbai-metro-gets-a-new-chembur-station-as-line-2b-expands-081426)); OpenStreetMap still notes that "Chembur is only a temporary terminus" ([relation 20403808](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/20403808)). The first four stations are inside the game map; Mankhurd and Mandale lie just east of its 72.920° edge. The unbuilt western and central part is mapped as under construction, and by position (an inferred order, from OpenStreetMap coordinates) it runs from D N Nagar through ESIC Nagar, Prem Nagar, Indira Nagar, Nanavati Hospital, Khira Nagar, Saraswat Nagar, National College, MMRDA, IL&FS, MTNL, SG Barve Marg, Kurla Terminal, Kurla and EEH (Eastern Express Highway, a future Line 4 interchange) to Chembur. All 14 of those stations are inside the map box; OpenStreetMap tags the remainder with an opening date of December 2027, and no official date exists.

## Line 1 runs at crush while Line 3 stays roomy

Adding up the per-line figures, on an ordinary weekday in late 2026 the metro carries about **11–12 lakh journeys**, against the suburban railway's 6.5–7 million ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Suburban_Railway)). **Line 1 carries about 5.5 lakh** ([Indian Infrastructure](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2026/03/06/digital-ticketing-building-a-future-ready-afc-system-for-mumbai-metro-line-1/)), set a record of **5,66,851 on 22 August 2025** ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-monorail-near-10-lakh-daily-ridership-milestone)), and **88 % of its riders travel between Ghatkopar and Andheri** ([Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-metro-line-1-update-faster-connectivity-on-ghatkopar-andheri-stretch-with-new-service-pattern-to-reduce-platform-crowding-a525/)). **Line 3 averaged 2,24,602 riders per weekday and 1,31,138 per weekend day in September 2026**, and logged 72,088,738 journeys in its first full year ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-line-3-boosts-train-frequency-with-46-additional-weekday-trips-amid-rising-passenger-demand); [Urban Acres](https://urbanacres.in/mumbai-metro-aqua-line-crosses-7-2-crore-journeys-in-first-year/)), still far below its forecast of about 1.5 million a day ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqua_Line_(Mumbai_Metro))). Lines 2A and 7 together carry about 3 lakh, with a record of 3,40,571 ([Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/transport/mumbai-metro-lines-2a-and-7-see-record-ridership-cross-3.4-lakh-mark-90036)), and MMMOCL's whole network (2A, 7, 9 and 2B) passed 30 crore journeys on 3 August 2026 ([Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/transport/mmrda-metro-crosses-300-million-passenger-journeys-marking-record-ridership-growth-in-mumbai-93816)). At the other extreme, Line 2B's stub averaged **3,885 riders a day** in its launch week, against 22,356 on Line 9 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-9-sees-strong-ridership-diamond-gardenmandale-metro-2b-lags)), and one report put it at only about 300 a day, which is why MMRDA rushed the Chembur station open ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/chembur-metro-station-in-mumbai-set-for-early-launch-as-line-2b-witnesses-low-ridership-report)). Before it shut, the Monorail carried about 18,000 a day ([Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/news-brief/mumbai-monorail-set-for-restart-after-months-long-shutdown-final-safety-clearance-expected-next-week)).

Dividing riders by seats tells the real story. Line 1 puts about 550,000 boardings into roughly 476 four-coach trips, **about 290 boardings per coach run**; Line 3 puts 224,602 into 337 eight-coach trips, **about 83**, so a Line 1 coach is roughly three and a half times as busy before even counting Line 3's longer line and higher turnover. At peak, Line 1 offers 18 trains an hour per direction; at about 1,750 people per train ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-1-may-soon-get-six-coach-trains-to-ease-rush-hour-crowds)) that is about 31,500 people an hour each way, barely above the 30,491 the line was forecast to need **by 2031** ([TCCA](https://tcca.info/consort-digital-to-upgrade-mumbai-metro-line-1-with-mcx-one/)). Line 1 therefore runs near crush now, which is why it is buying 22 coaches to lengthen trains to six cars. Line 3 at peak offers about 40,000 places an hour each way for a fraction of that demand, and viral comparisons contrast a "jam-packed" AC local with Line 3 passengers "comfortably seated", at a price: an Andheri–Churchgate air-conditioned local pass costs about ₹1,335 a month against about ₹3,500 on Line 3 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/pay-extra-for-peaceful-travel-netizens-react-as-commuter-compares-rates-between-ac-local-train-vs-mumbai-metro-3)).

No operator publishes an hourly ridership table, but the shape of the day is clear from what they do publish. **Ghatkopar alone handles up to 175,000 people a day, nearly 120,000 of them in the morning and evening peaks** ([Indian Infrastructure](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2026/03/06/digital-ticketing-building-a-future-ready-afc-system-for-mumbai-metro-line-1/)), so about 69 % of its movements fall in two windows of a few hours. Line 1's extra short-loop trains run precisely 6:23–8:23 pm, and Line 3's frequency boosts target the weekday peaks. Weekends are about **58 % of a weekday** on Line 3 (131,138 against 224,602). Rain pushes people off the roads and onto the metro: MMMOCL's network set six records in 33 days during the 2026 monsoon, peaking at over **3.73 lakh on 21 July** ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-sets-new-single-day-ridership-record-with-over-373-lakh-commuters-on-july-21)), roughly 20–25 % above an ordinary day, and kept spare trains on standby on every corridor ([Constructionworld](https://www.constructionworld.in/transport-infrastructure/metro-rail-and-railways-infrastructure/mumbai-metro-records-highest-single-day-ridership/93782)).

The crush points are the interchanges. At **Ghatkopar** a blogger describes crowds spilling onto the footbridge and a "tiny staircase" and exits that pass one person at a time ([The Idea-Smithy](https://ramyapandyan.com/a-noobs-guide-to-the-mumbai-metro/)), with auto-rickshaws blocking the road outside ([DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/mumbai/report-unplanned-development-spells-chaos-at-ghatkopar-station-2736462)). On Line 3 the busiest stations from October to December 2025 were **Marol Naka (16.45 lakh entries and exits, about 18,000 a day)**, CSMT (10.28 lakh), BKC (7.3 lakh), and Siddhivinayak and Santacruz (about 6.66 lakh each) ([Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/civic/mumbai-metro-3-records-over-46-lakh-passenger-footfalls-in-december-know-3-most-crowded-stations-91398)); since the line has grown by about half since then, Marol Naka plausibly handles around 25,000 a day now. The Line 7–Line 1 change at Gundavali and Western Express Highway is busy at rush hour and adds about five minutes on foot ([Mumbai Metro Route](https://mumbaimetroroute.com/routes/gundavali-to-andheri)), and in early 2023 Andheri (West) and Gundavali were Lines 2A and 7's busiest stations ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-andheri-west-and-gundavali-metro-stations-have-the-highest-footfalls)). Directions are not published, but geography suggests mornings flow toward Andheri East's offices, Marol, BKC, Worli and Fort, and evenings reverse. The commuter mix follows the map: office workers with laptop bags, students, airport travellers with wheeled suitcases at Terminals 1 and 2, and families heading to Siddhivinayak and Dadar on festival nights.

MMMOCL's lines use the same steps up to ₹50, then ₹70 for 36–42 km and ₹80 above 42 km ([MMMOCL fare table](https://www.mmmocl.co.in/fare-table.php)), with the 30–36 km step presumably ₹60; Gundavali–Kashigaon costs ₹50 and Dahisar (East)–Kashigaon ₹20 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/good-news-commuters-now-travel-from-andheri-to-kashigaon-at-rs-50-know-fares-on-newly-launched-mumbai-metro-line-9)), and the whole 2B stub costs ₹20, ₹10 for a short hop, against ₹90–100 by cab ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-line-2b-launched-travel-from-mankhurd-to-chembur-in-just-20-check-ticket-fare-here)). Line 1 charges ₹10–40 ([Mumbai Metro Times](https://www.mumbaimetrotimes.com/mumbai-metro/)), though route planners show ₹30 end to end ([Mumbai Metro Route](https://mumbaimetroroute.com/routes/versova-to-ghatkopar)) and its slab boundaries are unpublished. The Monorail charged ₹10–40 ([Free Press Journal, 2019](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-entire-monorail-stretch-operational)) and has proposed ₹10–70 for its restart ([Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/amp/en/transport/mumbai-monorail-fares-likely-to-see-hike-as-mmrda-plans-restart-after-nine-month-shutdown-93223)).

Tickets are mostly QR codes, on paper from counters and vending machines, on phones in each operator's app, and on WhatsApp for both Line 1 and the MMMOCL lines ([Reliance Mumbai Metro](https://www.reliancemumbaimetro.com/ticket-fares); [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/mumbai-metro-introduces-whatsapp-based-ticketing-system/story)). Bank-issued National Common Mobility Cards (NCMC) work at all of Line 1's **125 entry and 122 exit gates** ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-one-modernises-afc-system-integrates-unified-payment-option-for-enhanced-commuter-convenience)) and on Line 3 ([Metro Rail Route](https://metrorailroute.com/mumbai-metro/smart-card/)), while MMMOCL issues its own "Mumbai 1" card, topped up with ₹100–2,000 ([MMMOCL FAQ](https://www.mmmocl.co.in/faq)). On Lines 2A and 7 that card offers 45-trip and 60-trip monthly passes at 15 % and 20 % off for a fixed pair of stations ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-discounted-metro-train-passes-launched-for-line-2a-7-commuters)), plus a 25 % concession for senior citizens, disabled people and school students ([Zee News](https://zeenews.india.com/railways/mumbai-metro-to-offer-25-percent-fare-concession-on-line-2a-7-from-may-1-check-eligibility-2600911.html)); Line 1 sells WhatsApp trip passes and stored-value passes that live in phone wallets ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-one-launches-digital-passes-on-whatsapp-for-commuters)). The state's **"Mumbai One" app, launched on 8 October 2025, sells one QR ticket across 11 operators**, including Metro Lines 1, 2A, 3 and 7, the Monorail, suburban trains and city buses ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/pm-launches-mumbai-one-single-app-for-all-public-transport-tickets/2760053/)).

Entry means a security check: X-ray baggage scanners and frisking ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/mumbai-metro-aqua-line-3-fpj-sting-exposes-how-explosives-went-undetected-raising-major-security-concerns-see-pics)), with Line 1 limiting bags to 25 kg and 80 × 50 × 30 cm ([Reliance Mumbai Metro](https://reliancemumbaimetro.com/security)). **Eating is banned inside coaches**, as MMRC reminded riders after a viral video of a man snacking on Line 3 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/no-civic-sense-man-seen-eating-inside-mumbai-metro-3-sparks-debate-online-video)); a social-media post claims a ₹500 fine for filming on Line 3, but enforcement is unconfirmed ([X post](https://x.com/marinebharat/status/1979244208809816344)). Line 1 has reserved **one coach for women since about two months after its 2014 launch** ([DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/mumbai/report-mumbai-metro-separate-coach-for-women-2010894)); the coach position, and women's coaches on other lines, were not found. Line 3's most distinctive experience is the **mobile-network blackout underground**: after the full opening, Airtel, Jio and Vi users lost calls and Unified Payments Interface (UPI) payments because of a pricing dispute between MMRC and the telecom companies ([Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-metro-line-3-network-blackout-leaves-passengers-unable-to-make-upi-payments-or-calls-a525)), later partly fixed by a joint agreement ([Developing Telecoms](https://www.developingtelecoms.com/telecom-technology/wireless-networks/20396-mumbai-metro-mobile-connectivity-dispute-may-be-over.html)). The line has also had its weather moments: on **26 May 2025**, Mumbai's earliest monsoon onset in 75 years, muddy water filled the Acharya Atre Chowk station ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/05/26/bom56-mh-rain-ld-metro.html)), which MMRC blamed on a collapsed wall at an unfinished entrance ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-3-flooding-mmrc-issues-clarification-denies-tunnel-leak-after-rainwater-enters-acharya-atre-chowk-station)), after which entrances flush with the road got tarpaulins and makeshift walls ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/06/02/bes15-mh-rains-metro-rail-shelar.html)). For its first anniversary MMRC launched a commuter stamp trail and a time capsule to be opened in 2036 ([Urban Acres](https://urbanacres.in/mumbai-metro-aqua-line-crosses-7-2-crore-journeys-in-first-year/)).

The Monorail's story is the city's favourite cautionary tale. On **19 August 2025**, a heavy-rain day when the Harbour line was shut, two overcrowded trains stalled and **782 passengers were rescued** ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fmaharashtra%2F782-passengers-rescued-from-2-stranded-mumbai-monorails-3687768)); officials said one train had passed its **104-tonne weight limit** and cut its own power ([Daily Jagran](https://www.thedailyjagran.com/india/why-mumbais-monorail-got-stuck-between-chembur-and-bhakti-park-officials-reveal-10261445)). Service stopped on 20 September 2025 for a rebuild with **10 new Made-in-India four-car trains** (a ₹590 crore order from a Medha Servo Drives–SMH Rail joint venture) and communication-based train control (CBTC) signalling ([Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-made-in-india-rakes-with-21-new-features-set-to-transform-monorail-travel-a525/)). The CMRS gave final clearance in June 2026 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mmrda-gets-final-safety-clearance-for-monorail-seeks-state-nod-to-resume-services)), Power Mech Projects won the five-year operations contract for ₹296.4 crore ([Metro Rail Today](https://metrorailtoday.com/news/power-mech-projects-bags-296-crore-om-contract-for-mumbai-monorail-project)), yet in September 2026 MMRDA was still waiting for IIT Bombay's advice on covering the gaps between the two guideways ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-monorail-mmrda-awaits-iit-bombay-recommendation-to-cover-guideway-gaps-before-resuming-services)) and weighing safety nets that "may delay services by 3–4 months" ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-monorail-reopening-safety-net-installation-may-delay-services-by-3-4-months-mumbai-news)). No report of a restart was found up to mid-November 2026.

## U-girders at 13 metres and tunnels 5.8 metres wide

Line 3's interior theme, "Dynamic Fluidism", is "inspired by the vital flow of water", with beige for comfort and aqua green for freshness.

Elevated lines stand on **single piers in the road median carrying twin precast U-shaped girders**, one per track, whose side walls hide the wheels. On Line 2A the two-track deck is **10.55 m wide**, spans run up to 28 m, and **rail level is about 13 m above the road**, rising to about 21 m where 2A crosses Line 1 at D N Nagar ([NBMCW](https://www.nbmcw.com/product-technology/precast/mumbai-metro-line-2a-use-of-precast-construction-in-urban-infrastructure-projects.html)); Line 1 has many 26–30 m spans ([Scribd overview](https://www.scribd.com/presentation/438092507/Mumbai-Metro)), and a preliminary design gives a U-girder depth of 2.15 m ([Pedelta](https://www.pedelta.com/preliminary-design-of-viaduct-for-the-mumbai-metro-p-87-en)). Steel spans of 42–65 m leap railways and highways ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mmrda-achieves-major-engineering-milestone-on-mumbai-metro-line-6-completes-42m-steel-span-weighing-168-tonnes-over-western-railway)), Line 9 shares a one-kilometre double-decker with a road flyover in Mira–Bhayandar ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/maharashtra/first-double-decker-flyover-with-metro-viaduct-in-mumbai-metropolitan-region-opens-to-traffic-3167670)), and Line 2B crosses over the Monorail at Vashi Naka at about 18 m, 5.6 m above the monorail beam ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-news-metro-2b-to-ply-above-monorail-thanks-to-u-girder)). About **2,500 piers are painted in their line's colour** ([Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/transport/mumbai-metro-corridors-marked-with-2500-painted-pillars-as-per-decided-colour-codes-89446)), a cheap and recognisable detail.

The elevated stations on Lines 2A and 7 are **three-level buildings cantilevered off the median piers**: street, concourse, and **140 m side platforms about 6.45 m above the concourse** ([NBMCW](https://www.nbmcw.com/product-technology/precast/mumbai-metro-line-2a-use-of-precast-construction-in-urban-infrastructure-projects.html); [The Metro Rail Guy](https://themetrorailguy.com/2018/02/15/j-kumar-launches-1st-platform-pier-cap-for-mumbai-metro-line-2/)), with about **1.2 m-high glass platform screen doors** ([Free Press Journal](https://www.pressreader.com/india/the-free-press-journal/20210301/281801401691304)) that have suffered glitches ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/mumbai-metro-line-2a7-platform-screen-doors-at-metro-stations-report-glitch-watch-video)). Line 1's 12 stations have side platforms except Ghatkopar, which has an island ([Scribd overview](https://www.scribd.com/presentation/438092507/Mumbai-Metro)), and no source mentions screen doors on Line 1. From the cited heights, a game station box would be roughly 140–150 m long (six coaches) and 20–24 m wide, with a concourse about 7–8 m above the road and the platform at about 14 m; widths are estimates.

Line 3 was dug by **17 tunnel boring machines that cut about 55 km of twin single-track tunnel with a 5.8 m finished inside diameter** ([MMRC](https://corporate.mmrcl.com/sites/default/files/Press%20Release%20-%20MMRC%20achieves%20its%20Final%20Breakthrough%20at%20Mumbai%20Central%20Metro%20Station..pdf)), typically **15–25 m below the street** ([SYSTRA](https://www.systra.com/india/project/mumbai-metro-line-3/)) and about 30 m at Terminal 2, the deepest ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatrapati_Shivaji_Maharaj_International_Airport_-_T2_metro_station)). Twenty stations were built in open trenches roofed over ("cut and cover"), and seven (Hutatma Chowk, Kalbadevi, Girgaon, Grant Road, Shitala Devi, Santacruz and Marol Naka) were mined with the New Austrian Tunnelling Method ([TunnelTalk](https://tunneltalk.com/India-12Mar2020-Mumbai-Metro-Line-3-excavation-methods-advance-and-statistics.php)); the Cuffe Parade and BKC boxes reach 450 m with their crossovers ([Indian Infrastructure](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2026/07/03/tunnelling-through-complexity-mmrcls-experience-in-executing-indias-first-fully-underground-metro-corridor/)). Every station has platform screen doors ([Alstom](https://www.alstom.com/press-releases-news/2024/10/mumbai-metro-aqua-line-inaugurated-commercial-operation-trains-and-signalling-solutions-supplied-alstom)), and entrances are numbered by concourse side (A1, A2, B1…). Street-level design varies with heritage: **Hutatma Chowk's entrances are open to the sky in stone and glass**, without canopies, which drew complaints in the rain ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-news-mmrcl-faces-public-concern-over-open-air-design-at-hutatma-chowk-metro-station)), CSMT's entrance is a **glass dome that frames the old terminus** ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-3-first-glimpses-of-iconic-csmt-station-on-aqua-line-unveiled-check-out-visuals)), and Siddhivinayak has seven entrances ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddhivinayak_metro_station)). The Monorail's train straddles a concrete beam "two to three feet wide" at least 5.5 m above the road ([Scomi SUTRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scomi_SUTRA); [Mumbai Monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Monorail)), and each station has four escalators.

MMRDA stations use **black sign panels**, chosen with line colours to keep wayfinding language-independent ([Metro Rail News](https://metrorailnews.in/mmrda-unveiled-colours-for-metro-7-2a-signages/)); Line 3's name boards are in Marathi and English and its concourse signs in Marathi, Hindi and English ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/metro-3-phase-2a-nears-completion-station-name-boards-in-marathi-and-english-being-installed)). No official colour codes, typefaces, announcement scripts or door chimes were found; the operators confirm only that announcements play in stations and trains ([MMMOCL FAQ](https://www.mmmocl.co.in/faq)). The game should therefore record its own trilingual announcements and pick its own approximate line colours (blue, yellow, aqua, red, green) from photographs.

### The extracted data is game-ready; four engineering traps remain

The script [scripts/research/metro-from-osm.mjs](../../../../scripts/research/metro-from-osm.mjs) reads the local extract in about 3 seconds and writes [osm-metro.json](../osm-metro.json) (0.78 MB), using exactly the converter's projection. For each operating line it holds **both direction centrelines as polylines in game x, z, with no gaps**, every stop with its distance along the line, and its length inside the box; for each station, names including `name:mr` (98 of 108 operating stations) and `name:hi`, game position, level, building and **platform outlines (119)**, stop positions where trains should halt, **entrances (82, with refs such as A1 or "Gate 2B" and lifts)**, nearby metro and railway stations, and opening dates; for lines without clean route relations (2B remainder, 4, 4A, 6, 7A, 11 and others), merged main-track polylines; and a list of **46 data problems**, mostly name mismatches between a station and its stops (Line 7's "Poisar" vs "Mahindra & Mahindra", Line 3's "Science Centre" vs "Science Museum"). Coverage has holes the game must fill by hand: there are **no heights** (only OpenStreetMap layer numbers), **no platforms on Line 3 south of BKC or on Line 2B**, entrances at only 14 of Line 3's 27 stations and none on the Monorail or Line 2B, and no timetable tags except Line 1's interval of 5 and the Monorail's 22-minute interval and 42-minute run time.

Four traps in the existing code deserve attention before reuse. First, the converter currently **skips elevated metro and monorail entirely** (plan item 3.1 in [plan.md](../../../plan.md)), so viaducts, piers and stations need a new mesh path, with collidable piers in road medians where the game's traffic already drives. Second, the local trains in [src/sim/trains.js](../../../../src/sim/trains.js) are an excellent template (a run profile of accelerate, cruise, brake and dwell; trains placed by time alone; one instanced mesh for all coaches; tickets at a window; ride by fade), but their station-name function `stationName()` in [scripts/lib/trains.mjs](../../../../scripts/lib/trains.mjs) must not be reused unchanged: its pattern `chhatrapati shivaji` would rename both airport stations to "Chhatrapati Chai Terminus", and stripping brackets would merge Line 2A's "Andheri (West)" with Line 1's "Andheri" and erase the sides of "Goregaon (East)" and "Dahisar (East)", while Line 1's OpenStreetMap objects carry an " L1" suffix ("Andheri L1", "Ghatkopar L1"). Third, the game clock runs **60 game seconds per real second** ([clock.js](../../../../src/sim/clock.js)), so real headways cannot be both physical and clock-true: the local trains move in real seconds with a 150-second peak interval and let the clock only thin the timetable, which means a 3-hour morning peak lasts 3 real minutes and a waiting player sees about one Line 1 train per peak. The metro should follow the same convention and let **crowd density, not frequency, carry the rush hour**. Fourth, a ride by fade should advance the clock by the real journey time, about **2 game minutes per station** (55 for Line 3 end to end, about 22 for Line 1), which matches the locals' 2.3 minutes per station.

### A suggested weekday timetable and crowd table

The table below is a design proposal built from the cited figures, not an official schedule. Intervals are in real seconds of play per the local-train convention (Line 1 peak 200 s, Line 3 peak 265 s), and the clock thins them. The **crowd index** shapes the day so that about 59 % of riders travel in the two peak windows, a little flatter than Ghatkopar's 69 %; hourly riders are the daily total × index ÷ 10.3. People per coach are estimated at the busiest section as hourly riders ÷ trains per hour (both directions) × share of boarders aboard at the busiest point (0.8 on Line 1, where 88 % ride Ghatkopar–Andheri; an assumed 0.45 on the long Line 3) × 1.2 in the peak direction ÷ coaches per train. Crush is about **440 per coach on Line 1** (1,750 ÷ 4) and **375 on Line 3** (3,000 ÷ 8). With these intervals the model runs about 456 Line 1 trips and 347 Line 3 trips a day, close to the reported ~476 and 337.

The numbers say two things the screen should show. A Line 1 coach is never comfortable: even at midday it carries 200–300 people, every seat taken and the aisles full, rising to crush in the peak direction on Ghatkopar–Andheri, while the Versova end is about half as full. A Line 3 coach at peak holds roughly its seat count (about 50 per coach on the comparable six-coach trains, 298 ÷ 6) plus a few standing, and is half empty at midday, perhaps twice as busy between Marol Naka, BKC and Dadar. Since the game cannot draw 440 people in a coach, it should map fullness (load ÷ crush) to visible figures packed around doors and windows, and put the real numbers into platform queues: at Ghatkopar's morning peak about 20,000 people an hour pass through, so roughly 500–600 board each departing train at this terminus. For **weekends**, multiply riders by about 0.58, flatten the curve into a broad hump from late morning to evening, and use off-peak intervals all day (Saturday busier than Sunday is a plausible but unsourced split). For **rain**, multiply metro crowds by about 1.2 and thin road traffic; for **New Year's Eve**, run Line 3 all night, add late Line 1 trains and 15-minute late trains on the 2A and 7 stubs. The Monorail, if the owner chooses to run it, would use an 8-minute interval from 6:00 am to 10:00 pm with light loads (about 18,000 riders a day before it shut).

### A sensible build order, and what is still unknown

The research points to a clear order. **Line 3 first, as underground fast travel**, which plan item 3.4 already lists: surface entrances from the 45 mapped entrance points (the station node elsewhere), one generic interior (concourse, gates, escalators, island platform with screen doors) built once with the [interiors](../../../../src/world/interiors.js) kit and reused for all 27 stations with swapped name boards, fares from the distance slabs, and a fade ride of about 2 game minutes per station. **Line 1 second, with visible trains**: 11.2 km of U-girder viaduct at 13 m on median piers, 12 station boxes, and two set pieces, the Ghatkopar crush and the Marol Naka change to Line 3. **The Monorail third**, beams and stations first for the skyline, run either closed with parked trains, true to November 2026, or reopened as a story event, since the design principles treat reality as a starting point. **The edge stubs last**: Line 2A and Line 7 at Andheri and Line 2B at Chembur, fading out at the map edge until the map grows, plus unfinished Line 6 pillars near the player's Powai home and Line 4 pillars along the Eastern Express Highway as construction scenery.

Several things remain unknown and should stay configurable. No operator publishes hourly ridership or weekend intervals, so the crowd table above is a model. Line 1's fare boundaries are unpublished (₹30 or ₹40 end to end). Exact coach lengths, door counts and heights, station widths, Line 3's screen-door height, tunnel spacing and station depths beyond Terminal 2 were not found, nor were Line 1's and the new Monorail trains' liveries, any announcement wording, or women's-coach positions beyond Line 1. Whether the Monorail restarted between September and mid-November 2026, and whether Line 4's first phase opens in December, are unconfirmed. Interchange walkways (the Andheri skywalk, the D N Nagar bridge, the 58 m Gundavali footbridge) are not mapped as geometry. Finally, most web facts came from search extracts rather than full pages, so any number that will be shown to players (fares, timings) deserves a spot check when the sites are reachable.

## Conclusion

The metro is the cheapest large system the game can add next, because the hard part, an accurate 1:1 map of every line, already exists in the project's own data, and the three lines inside the box are self-contained. Line 3 in particular needs almost no world geometry, only entrances, one reusable interior and a fade, yet it turns a 33 km north–south crossing into a two-minutes-per-station hop between most of the places missions already use. The real world also supplies the gameplay contrast for free: Line 1 is cheap, fast and brutally crowded, Line 3 costs more and lets you sit, which is exactly the "pay extra for peace" trade-off Mumbai commuters argue about.

The less obvious lesson is about time. Because the clock runs sixty times faster than play while trains move at real speed, rush hour cannot be sold by train frequency, which would either jam the tracks or leave players watching empty platforms. It has to be sold by people: queues on the Ghatkopar footbridge at 9 am, a packed door zone on Line 1 at 7 pm, an almost empty Line 3 coach at 3 pm. And the city's own unfinished business, a monorail that keeps "restarting next month", an underground line with no phone signal, a brand-new stub line with a few hundred riders, gives the game satire that needs no invention at all.
