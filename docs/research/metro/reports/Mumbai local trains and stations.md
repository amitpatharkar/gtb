# Mumbai's local trains pulse every three minutes

In November 2026 Mumbai's suburban railway runs about **3,250 trains every weekday** (Western Railway about 1,414, Central Railway about 1,820 across the Central, Harbour, Trans-Harbour and Port lines) and carries roughly **70–75 lakh (7–7.5 million) journeys** on an ordinary working day, with peak trains leaving each terminus **every 2–3 minutes** and running at about twice their rated load into town in the morning and out of town in the evening. The trains are 12-car (and, on about one Western service in six, 15-car) electric multiple units (EMUs) of **20.73 m coaches, 3.66 m wide**, stopping about **20 seconds** at each station, or close to a minute for the air-conditioned (AC) rakes, which now make up about a tenth of services. Second-class fares have not changed since 2022 (**₹5 to about ₹20** for 60 km), there are still no fare gates, and since 1 July 2026 the minimum penalty for travelling without a ticket is **₹500 plus the fare**. For Bombay Bawaal the most useful finding is that the hard geometry already exists: the project's own OpenStreetMap extract, processed into [osm-stations.json](../osm-stations.json), holds every one of the **43 railway stations inside the map box (27 in South Mumbai)** with platform outlines, numbers, the tracks along each edge, 124 footbridge spans, 66 roof pieces and middle cross-sections, all in game coordinates; together with [osm-metro.json](../osm-metro.json) for the metro, it lets the owner build stations at their real size and place. What the map cannot give (platform height, footbridge and roof heights, canopy design, coach order, sounds) comes from the sourced figures below, and the honest unknowns are listed at the end. Most web facts in this report come from **search-engine extracts of the cited pages**, because the cloud sandbox could not open Wikipedia, the railway websites or most news sites; station positions, distances and track layouts were instead measured directly from the OpenStreetMap extract of 13 November 2026, and service patterns from a real 2023–24 timetable.

## Five corridors, measured station by station along the mapped track

Three kinds of evidence sit behind this report, and they deserve different levels of trust. **Distances and platform layouts were measured** from the project's OpenStreetMap extract (`data/raw/mumbai-region.osm.pbf`, extract.bbbike.org, 13 November 2026), along the mapped slow track from each terminus buffer stop; they agree with official chainage to within about 1 km (Churchgate–Virar 60.2 km, CSMT–Kalyan 53.7 km against an official 54, CSMT–Panvel 49.0 against 49). **Stopping patterns, hourly departures and run times on the Western line** come from the real Western Railway timetable published on trainhelp.in, scraped into CSV files and committed to GitHub in January 2024 ([Umang-Lodaya/Mumbai-Local-TimeTable-Extractor](https://github.com/Umang-Lodaya/Mumbai-Local-TimeTable-Extractor/tree/ba5005759da90af0fbd8b8988f9863b1629fef11/data)); its 199 trains in the 92xxx series match the 199 15-car services Western Railway announced from 15 August 2023, so it is the late-2023 timetable, and the researchers parsed about 90 % of its trains (counts are ±10 %). It is called "the 2023–24 timetable" below. **Everything else from the web came from search-result extracts**, not full pages, and single-source numbers should be checked before they are shown to players. Station codes are the OpenStreetMap `ref` tags; CSMT stands for Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, which the game already shows as **"Chhatrapati Chai Terminus"**.

## About 3,250 trains a day run on a three-minute heartbeat

Western Railway runs about **1,414 suburban services per weekday** after a February 2026 revision, of which **145–147 are AC** since May–September 2026, up from 79 in 2023; its 15-car services numbered about 211–213 in 2024 plus 17 conversions on 1 May 2026, roughly 16 % of all trains ([Free Press Journal, Feb 2026](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-western-railway-to-introduce-12-more-ac-local-trains-3-non-ac-services-from-19th-feb); [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-local-train-update-wr-expands-15-coach-ac-services-across-suburban-network-from-may-1-a525/); [Kordinate](https://kordinate.world/western-railway-mumbai-local-train-september-2026-schedule/)). Central Railway runs about **1,820** across its four lines, of which **120 are AC (92 main line, 28 Harbour)** since 29 June 2026, and its 15-car services rose from 32 to **54 on 15 August 2026**, all on the main line ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/central-railway-converts-12-non-ac-local-services-into-ac-trains-from-june-29); [Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/mumbai/mumbai-central-railway-increases-ac-local-services-to-120-with-12-new-trains-69201823551); [Free Press Journal, 15-car](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/central-railway-to-add-22-more-15-coach-locals-from-august-15-to-ease-main-line-crowding)). The Harbour line runs about 608 services, the Trans-Harbour line about 252 (2021 figure) and the Uran line 50 ([Harbour line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_line_(Mumbai_Suburban_Railway)); [Trans-Harbour line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Harbour_line_(Mumbai_Suburban_Railway))). Subtracting those from Central's total leaves roughly 900 main-line trains, so **about one main-line train in ten and one Harbour train in twenty is AC**, and almost all new AC services replaced ordinary ones rather than adding to them.

### Slow, fast, semi-fast and "double fast": who stops where

On the Western line in the 2023–24 timetable, **62 % of Churchgate departures were slow (all stations) and 38 % fast**. The standard fast train calls at Churchgate, Marine Lines, Charni Road, Grant Road, Mumbai Central, Dadar, Bandra, Andheri and Borivali, then usually every station to Virar (113 trains northbound a day); a "direct" fast also skips Marine Lines, Charni Road and Grant Road (38 a day); and a **semi-fast** runs fast to Andheri and then calls at Jogeshwari, Goregaon, Malad and Kandivali, skipping Ram Mandir (22 a day, about one fast train in eight). North of Borivali about 85–90 % of trains stop everywhere, and the rest, the "double fast", call only at Bhayandar and Vasai Road (22 northbound). The commonest services are Churchgate–Borivali slow (168 northbound a day), Churchgate–Virar fast (98), Churchgate–Borivali fast (49) and Churchgate–Goregaon slow (47), plus 18 Dadar–Virar fasts, 11 of them 15-car ([2023–24 timetable](https://github.com/Umang-Lodaya/Mumbai-Local-TimeTable-Extractor/tree/ba5005759da90af0fbd8b8988f9863b1629fef11/data)). Fifteen-car trains in that timetable ran on fast runs calling only at the fast halts and on all-stations runs between Andheri and Virar; **no 15-car train called at the slow-only stations between Mumbai Central and Vile Parle**, and the Bandra–Andheri slow corridor is only due to take 15-car trains from January 2027 ([Navbharat Live](https://navbharatlive.com/maharashtra/mumbai/western-railway-15-coach-local-andheri-bandra-platform-expansion-1461158.html/amp)).

On the Central main line, fast trains call at **CSMT, Byculla, Dadar, Kurla, Ghatkopar, Thane, Dombivli and Kalyan**, with some peak trains adding Vikhroli, Bhandup, Mulund or Diva; trains for Kasara, Karjat, Khopoli, Badlapur, Ambarnath and Titwala usually run fast to Kalyan and slow beyond, and a few "semi-fast" trains add or skip stops ([Central line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_line_(Mumbai_Suburban_Railway)); [OpenStreetMap 9953730](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/9953730); [RailYatri 95417](https://www.railyatri.in/time-table/95417)). Inside the game map that means **fast trains skip Masjid, Sandhurst Road, Chinchpokli, Currey Road, Parel, Matunga, Sion and Vidyavihar**. No source used the term "double fast" for Central Railway.

### First trains at 03:25, last at 01:00, and a three-hour night gap

The famous first Western train leaves **Churchgate at 04:15 for Virar** and the last at **01:00 for Borivali**; the first southbound train leaves Virar at 03:25 and the last Virar–Churchgate at 00:05, arriving 01:45 ([Churchgate railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchgate_railway_station); [2023–24 timetable](https://github.com/Umang-Lodaya/Mumbai-Local-TimeTable-Extractor/tree/ba5005759da90af0fbd8b8988f9863b1629fef11/data)). AC trains leave Churchgate from about 05:45 to 21:57. On Central Railway the last CSMT–Kasara local leaves at **00:08** and the last CSMT–Karjat at **00:12** under the timetable from 5 October 2024, the last Harbour train to Panvel at about 00:13, and the first CSMT departures are quoted at around 04:00–04:30 by aggregators but not confirmed ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-central-railway-announces-new-suburban-train-timetable-from-october-5-check-timings-last-train-departure-platform-shift-details-here); [indiarailinfo 98001](https://indiarailinfo.com/train/timetable/mumbai-csmt-panvel-slow-local-98001/15744/12282/1250); [MumbaiLocal.Info](https://mumbaimetro.info/local-trains/harbour)). In town, then, there is a **3–4 hour window with no trains**, which is when maintenance happens.

Western Railway's own presentation gives a **3-minute peak headway** on its suburban section ([Press Information Bureau presentation](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/MTRC_PPT.pdf)), and a July 2026 report says the current signalling is close to capacity: the planned Kavach 5.0 cab-signalling system aims at **2-minute headways** and lifting daily services from 3,254 to nearly 4,000 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-local-trains-set-for-major-safety-upgrade-as-railways-develop-kavach-50-to-boost-capacity)).

### Run times and speeds

Median run times on the Western line are **Churchgate–Dadar 21 minutes slow / 17 fast, Churchgate–Andheri 44 / 32, Churchgate–Borivali 65 / 46, and Churchgate–Virar 103 / 83**, with the double fast at 77 and Churchgate–Dahanu Road about 2 h 37 min; fast sections between halts run Churchgate–Mumbai Central 8 minutes, Mumbai Central–Dadar 7, Dadar–Bandra 6, Bandra–Andheri 9 and Andheri–Borivali 14 ([2023–24 timetable](https://github.com/Umang-Lodaya/Mumbai-Local-TimeTable-Extractor/tree/ba5005759da90af0fbd8b8988f9863b1629fef11/data)). With the measured distances, that is an **average of about 32 km/h for a slow train and 45 km/h for a fast one, stops included**, and about 52 km/h on the non-stop Andheri–Borivali run. Central Railway's CSMT–Kalyan slow takes **1 h 28–30 min (about 37 km/h)** and the fast about 60 minutes (about 54 km/h); CSMT–Panvel takes about 1 h 17–20 min, CSMT–Goregaon 53–56 minutes and Vashi–Thane 29 minutes ([indiarailinfo K63](https://d.indiarailinfo.com/train/20608); [indiarailinfo 98027](https://indiarailinfo.com/train/timetable/mumbai-csmt-panvel-slow-local-98027/15637/12282/1250); [indiarailinfo 99500](https://indiarailinfo.com/train/-train-vashi-thane-slow-local-99500/19232/9362/467)). That works out to roughly **2–3 minutes per station hop south of Borivali**, dwell included.

### Sundays, mega blocks, monsoons and festival nights

Sunday service is a thinned weekday timetable driven by notes on individual trains: "NOT ON SUNDAYS & HOLIDAYS" (about 12 % of Western trains), "ON SUN ONLY", ladies specials that run as general trains on Sunday, and AC slots worked by ordinary rakes at weekends ([2023–24 timetable](https://github.com/Umang-Lodaya/Mumbai-Local-TimeTable-Extractor/tree/ba5005759da90af0fbd8b8988f9863b1629fef11/data)); Western Railway's weekend AC count was 118 against 145 on weekdays from May 2026, and Central Railway's 2026 AC conversions revert to non-AC rakes on Sundays and holidays ([Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-local-train-update-wr-expands-15-coach-ac-services-across-suburban-network-from-may-1-a525/); [Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/mumbai/mumbai-central-railway-increases-ac-local-services-to-120-with-12-new-trains-69201823551)). Almost every Sunday Central Railway closes one main-line and one Harbour section from about **11:00 to 16:00**: on 8 November 2026 the slow lines Matunga–Mulund were blocked 11:05–15:55, slow trains were diverted to the fast lines running about 15 minutes late, and Harbour trains were cut between Kurla and Vashi with specials on either side ([Newsband](https://www.newsband.in/article_detail/central-railway-to-operate-mega-block-on-main-and-harbour-lines-on-sunday); [Lokshahi](https://english.lokshahi.com/maharashtra/mumbai-local-alert-mega-block-on-central-railway-this-sunday-multiple-trains-cancelled-check-updated-timings-passengers-advised-to-plan-travel-ahead-11867791)). A "jumbo block" on Central, Harbour and Trans-Harbour lines is announced for **Sunday 15 November 2026**, the day after this report ([eSakal](https://esakal.com/mumbai/sunday-15th-november-mumbai-local-train-jumbo-block-impact-on-central-harbour-and-trans-harbour-services-mvk02)). Western Railway usually prefers night blocks, though it also blocked on 25 October 2026 ([Newsband](https://www.newsband.in/article_detail/cr-mega-block-on-sunday-wr-to-conduct-night-block); [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-local-train-mega-block-on-october-25-2026-services-on-central-harbour-and-western-lines-to-be-disrupted-on-sunday-check-details-a517/amp/)).

In heavy rain the classic trouble spots are **Sion–Kurla on the main line and Chunabhatti–Kurla on the Harbour line**, both inside the game map, where water rises about 8 inches above the rails as the Mithi river swells; on 19 August 2025 the Harbour line was suspended between CSMT and Kurla, and Central Railway now runs 210 dewatering pumps at 117 locations ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-monsoon-fury-relentless-rains-paralyse-central-railway-thousands-stranded); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fmaharashtra%2Fmumbai-rains-local-train-services-on-harbour-line-suspended-due-to-flooded-tracks-3687042); [MumbaiLive](https://www.mumbailive.com/amp/en/civic/cr-installs-210-heavy-duty-dewatering-pumps-at-117-waterlogging-prone-locations-93361)). On Anant Chaturdashi night (Ganesh immersion, 6–7 September 2025) Western Railway ran six extra pairs of Churchgate–Virar trains after midnight and changed stops at Charni Road for Girgaon Chowpatty ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/ganeshotsav-2025-western-railway-announces-extra-services-schedule-changes-at-charni-road-for-anant-chaturdashi)).

### A machine-readable 2026 timetable exists

The most complete current timetable is the database behind the m-Indicator app, published as a 1.3 MB file (`cdn.mobond.com/mi/mumbaidb.zip`, version 20260417), which an MIT-licensed decoder turns into trains, stop times, **platform numbers and the side the doors open (left, right or both)** for about 5,714 trains network-wide ([jollySleeper BUILD_SPEC.md](https://github.com/jollySleeper/mumbai-local-timetable/blob/efa20a3419d7dfac3325531eaffd9036a1add198/BUILD_SPEC.md)). The data is a proprietary compilation with no stated licence, so it should be downloaded in a GitHub Action (the sandbox could not reach it), used only to derive patterns and door sides, and never shipped.

## Peak trains carry twice their rated load, inbound at 9 and outbound at 7

On an ordinary weekday in 2025–26 the network carries about **28–31 lakh journeys on the Western line and 40–45 lakh on Central Railway's lines**, roughly 70–75 lakh in all; Western ridership is still below its pre-pandemic 34.87 lakh, which reports blame on work from home and new metro lines, and the record day, **5 January 2026**, saw 72.18 lakh on Central and 52.07 lakh on Western, about 1.7 times normal ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-suburban-rail-earns-more-despite-lower-ridership-post-pandemic-travel-shift); [Geosquare](https://www.geosquare.in/mumbais-suburban-railway-surges-as-daily-ridership-nears-1-crore-37155-news.html); [RailPost](https://www.railpost.in/cr-transports-over-1-37-billion-people-in-fy26-to-date/)). About **two-thirds of riders hold season tickets**, so most peak riders are regulars who know exactly where their coach stops ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-pass-holder-but-want-to-travel-by-ac-train-you-may-soon-be-able-to-do-so)). Western AC locals carried about 1.5–1.6 lakh a day in 2025, about 5 % of riders on about 8–10 % of services ([Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/civic/mumbai-ac-local-sees-rise-in-footfall-as-heat-soars-84289)).

The railway's own term for peak trains is **"super dense crush load": 14–16 standing people per square metre**, coaches built for about 200 carrying over 500 ([Wikipedia, Crush load](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crush_load)). A 12-car rake is planned for **3,504 people (1,168 seated)**, about 2,400 is comfortable, and peak loads run above 6,000, with surveys finding up to 7,463 ([Mumbai Suburban Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Suburban_Railway); [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/mumbai-suburban-railway-problem-best-brts-solution/); [Academia.edu survey](https://www.academia.edu/95353066/Survey_of_Passenger_Ridership_in_Mumbai_Suburban_Railway)). The often-quoted "1,700 capacity, 5,000 aboard" belongs to the old nine-car era ([Vikalpa, 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0256090920110105)). Per coach of a 12-car train that is about 95–100 seated, 290 rated and **450–550 at peak**.

Peaks run about **7:30–11:00 towards Churchgate and CSMT and 17:00–21:00 away from town**, with the evening rush on the Virar side lasting to about 22:30 ([Mumbai77](https://www.mumbai77.com/city/6209/travel/local-train-travel-things-to-know/); [Mapa Metro](https://mapa-metro.com/en/India/Mumbai/Mumbai-Suburban-Railway-map.htm); [World Bank working paper](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/152531468268835999/pdf/wps4395.pdf)). No operator publishes hourly counts, so the following load factors (people ÷ 3,500 rated per 12-car train) are a model derived from the figures above, not a measurement.

By station, **Thane and Kalyan are the busiest** by tickets sold (about 93 million and 84 million originating journeys in 2023–24), followed by CSMT, Andheri, Dadar, Dombivli, Borivali and Churchgate; an older survey put Thane at 6.54 lakh a day, CSMT 6.37 lakh and Andheri 6.04 lakh, and Parel handles about 1.5 lakh ([List of busiest railway stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_railway_stations); [Rail Mitra](https://www.railmitra.com/blog/busiest-railway-station-in-india); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/thane-is-mumbais-most-crowded-railway-station-1.1192542); [LatestLY](https://www.latestly.com/india/information/mumbai-local-update-new-platform-parel-station-commissioned-from-today-to-decongest-rush-during-peak-hours-220264.html/amp)). Since the big origin stations (Virar, Borivali, Thane, Kalyan) lie outside the map, **trains should enter the map already full in the morning and leave full in the evening**, while Dadar, Kurla, Bandra and Andheri are scrums in both directions and the small South Mumbai stations (Marine Lines, Charni Road, Grant Road, Masjid, Sandhurst Road, Reay Road, Cotton Green) see mass alighting in the morning and boarding in the evening. Foot overbridges (FOBs) are the choke points: when several trains arrive together, the stairs are the slowest part of a station. A **brief safety fact:** in September 2017 a crowd crush on the narrow footbridge between Elphinstone Road and Parel stations killed 23 people, after which the Army built three wider footbridges and the railways began widening others ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2017/9/29/india-at-least-22-killed-in-mumbai-station-stampede); [Wikipedia, 2017 Mumbai stampede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Mumbai_stampede)).

The riding culture is built around regulars. **Door groups** hold the doorway in peak hours and control who boards, especially on Virar-bound trains, and one Western Railway drive against door blockers north of Borivali caught nearly 1,000 people ([Free Press Journal campaign](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/fpj-campaign-train-door-blocking-rampant-groupism-rife-in-suburban-locals-as-bullies-stand-guard); [Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/transport/strict-action-against-passengers-blocking-doors-in-trains-34284)). Skilled commuters squeeze an unofficial **"fourth seat"** onto three-seat benches ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/why-resumption-of-mumbai-local-trains-marks-return-to-normal-for-city-not-just-its-commute/599618/)); nearly **200 bhajan groups** sing devotional songs with cymbals on their regular trains, and a Railway Minister refused to ban them in 2018 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ani/bhajan-mandalis-cannot-be-stopped-from-playing-in-mumbai-locals-goyal-118011400035_1.html)); card players spread a newspaper across their laps in the men's compartments. **Dabbawalas** load wooden crates of about 40 tiffins (about 60 kg) into the luggage compartment in under 40 seconds and asked in vain for luggage space on AC trains, and Koli fisherwomen carry baskets in the same compartment ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/how-mumbais-dabbawalas-deliver-200000-meals); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-dabbawalas-demand-luggage-compartments-in-ac-trains); [Bombaylives](https://bombaylives.substack.com/p/luggage-dabba-bombay-local-train)). Western Railway ran the **world's first ladies special train in 1992**, both railways still run them, and ladies compartments see their own arguments over space ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/as-worlds-first-ladies-special-train-completes-30-years-central-and-western-railways-to-run-women-special-trains)). Hawkers, beggars and transgender people asking for money are a constant presence despite police drives (8,511 hawkers and 981 transgender persons booked in the first quarter of 2026); they belong in the game as ordinary parts of the train, never as jokes or targets ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/rpf-conducts-massive-crackdown-on-illegal-hawkers-and-beggars-in-mumbai-suburban-railways)).

Two police forces work the railway: the Railway Protection Force (RPF) for railway property, trespassers, hawkers and door blockers, and the Government Railway Police (GRP) for crime and accidents. About **2,287 people died on the suburban network in 2025**, down 7 % from 2024; about half were crossing tracks and 525 fell from moving trains, which is why fences, warning signs and police whistles, not accidents, are the right visual language ([Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/civic/mumbai-local-train-fatality-falls-7-in-2025-track-crossing-and-overcrowding-main-reasons-for-death-91328)). Door-hanging should be visible on ordinary peak trains but never rewarded or made a stunt, and it is absent on AC and automatic-door rakes.

## A ₹5 ticket and a ₹500 fine, with no gates in between

### Local train fares have been frozen since 2022

Suburban fares were last changed in **May 2022**, when AC single fares were halved (the AC minimum fell from ₹65 to ₹30 base for 5 km) and first-class singles were cut, with Churchgate–Virar first class becoming ₹100; the national fare rises of 1 July 2025 and 26 December 2025 both **exempted suburban tickets and season tickets** ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-ac-local-fare-cut-to-come-in-effect-from-today); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/railways-implements-revised-fare-for-ac-local-trains-and-first-class-travel-in-mumbai/943971/); [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/national/train-fare-hike-from-december-26-long-distance-travel-to-get-slightly-costlier-suburban-services-exempt-check-details-inside-a525/)). No official kilometre-slab table could be opened, but station fare charts and reports agree on the shape: **second class ₹5 (Churchgate to Mahim, CSMT to Dadar), ₹10 (Bandra, Matunga onward), ₹15 (Dahisar to Vasai Road) and ₹20 to Virar**, about 30 paise a kilometre; first class from about ₹25; AC from ₹35 as charged (₹35 for Byculla–CSMT or Dadar–CSMT, Churchgate–Virar ₹100–115, sources conflict) ([Amazing Maharashtra, Western chart](https://www.amazingmaharashtra.com/2014/09/local-train-ticket-fare-chart-mumbai.html); [Amazing Maharashtra, CSMT chart](https://www.amazingmaharashtra.com/2022/01/mumbai-csmt-station-ac-local-train-fare.html); [Bharatnama](https://bharatnama.substack.com/p/85-why-does-a-60-km-mumbai-local); [Mapa Metro](https://mapa-metro.com/en/India/Mumbai/Mumbai-Suburban-Railway-map.htm); [DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/mumbai/report-ac-mumbai-local-trains-fares-reduced-by-50-starting-today-check-new-ticket-prices-2950656)).

Season tickets come **monthly, quarterly (2.7 × monthly), half-yearly (5.4 ×) and yearly (10.8 ×)**; the second-class monthly starts at ₹100 for the shortest bands, the first-class monthly at ₹325, and AC monthlies from Churchgate run from ₹620 (Mumbai Central) to ₹2,205 (Virar) ([Wonderful Mumbai](https://wonderfulmumbai.com/latest-railway-ticket-fare-for-mumbai-local-and-outstation-trains/); [Mapa Metro](https://mapa-metro.com/en/India/Mumbai/Mumbai-Suburban-Railway-map.htm); [Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/transport/50-ac-local-fare-cut-to-come-in-effect-from-today-73084)). First-class pass holders on longer passes can upgrade to AC by paying the difference at a booking supervisor's window ([Inshorts](https://inshorts.com/en/news/firstclass-passengers-in-mumbai-can-upgrade-passes-to-travel-in-ac-locals-cr-1662458166310)). A **tourist ticket** gives unlimited travel on the Central, Western and Harbour lines for 1, 3 or 5 days at ₹75 / ₹115 / ₹135 in second class and ₹270 / ₹440 / ₹510 in first class (April 2026 listing; an older page gives ₹80 / ₹125 / ₹150), valid until midnight of the last day ([Audiala](https://audiala.com/en/india/mumbai/city-passes/); [TrainHelp](https://trainhelp.in/mumbai-local-train-tourist-ticket-pass/)). A **platform ticket costs ₹10 for 2 hours** at the issuing station only, does not allow boarding, and is temporarily raised to ₹50 at big stations during crowd surges; commuters then buy a ₹5 journey ticket instead ([eRail](https://erail.in/info/information-platform-ticket); [Knocksense](https://www.knocksense.com/mumbai/platform-ticket-rates-raised-to-50-at-key-stations-in-mumbai-metropolitan-region); [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/maharashtra/platform-tickets-cost-rs-50-check-out-the-jugaad-passengers-are-using-to-save-money/amp)). Children under 5 travel free and those aged 5–12 pay half, and students get free second-class monthly passes between home and school or college ([Rajya Sabha answer](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/624905/2/PQ_229_23082013_U1681_p307_p308.pdf); [Lok Sabha answer](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/605414/1/107836.pdf)).

### Rules: start within an hour, show it or it does not exist

A journey ticket, whether bought at a window, printed from an automatic ticket vending machine (ATVM) or booked on a phone, must be used to **start the journey within one hour** of issue; a paperless ticket cannot be cancelled; and "if the passenger is not able to show the ticket to the ticket checking staff, it should be considered ticketless travel" ([UTS on Mobile FAQ](https://www.utsonmobile.indianrail.gov.in/RDS/policy/faq_)). A return ticket's return half is valid until midnight of the following day ([Western Railway](https://wr.indianrailways.gov.in/view_section.jsp?lang=0&id=0%2C6%2C630)). No source spelled out Mumbai's "via" rules for journeys across lines.

### Where tickets come from in November 2026

A commuter can queue at a booking window (cash or UPI, the Unified Payments Interface for bank transfers by phone), use an ATVM by smart card or by scanning a UPI QR (Quick Response) code on its screen (Western Railway is adding 325 new machines to its 501), buy from **M-UTS Sahayaks**, staff who sell phone tickets to people in the queue, or use the railway's phone app ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-local-train-ticket-queues-to-reduce-as-western-railway-adds-325-digital-atvms); [Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/transport/mumbai-commuters-ditch-smart-cards-prefer-upi-for-local-train-tickets-88089); [Mumbai Live, sahayaks](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/civic/over-1.97-lakh-passengers-benefit-from-m-uts-sahayak-scheme-at-csmt-in-3-months-91622)). The old UTS (Unreserved Ticketing System) app stopped selling passes in January 2026 and was **shut on 1 March 2026**, replaced by the RailOne app ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/uts-train-ticket-booking-app-to-be-discontinued-in-mumbai-in-two-days-how-to-transition-to-railone-easily)). The key real-life mechanic is the **geo-fence**: since April–May 2024 a paperless ticket can be booked from anywhere except **inside station premises or within about 30 m of the tracks**, which is exactly where people remember they have no ticket ([Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/civic/external-border-geo-fencing-restrictions-lifted-for-paperless-ticket-booking-through-uts-app-83825); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/mumbai-railways-aims-to-stop-ticketless-travel-using-dynamic-qr-codes)). Station QR codes let hurried passengers book anyway, and some commuters saved those codes and scanned them only when a checker walked in, which the railways answered with **changing (dynamic) QR codes** ([Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/misuse-of-uts-app-on-mumbai-locals-railway-seeks-closure-of-qr-code-facility-to-prevent-ticket-fraud-a475)). The state's city-wide "Mumbai One" app, launched in October 2025, sells single-journey QR tickets for locals, metro, monorail and buses but no local season or return tickets ([Musafir](https://in.musafir.com/blog/mumbai-one-app); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/pm-launches-mumbai-one-single-app-for-all-public-transport-tickets/2760053/)). **There are still no fare gates at local stations**: Western Railway's plan for metro-style gates at a few stations is about 1.5 years old with no confirmed result, and only CSMT screens luggage airport-style at some entries ([Knocksense](https://www.knocksense.com/mumbai/metro-style-gates-coming-soon-to-these-3-mumbai-local-stations-details/); [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-csmt-enforces-airport-style-luggage-screening-amid-security-concerns-a525)).

### Checkers, cameras and the 2026 penalty law

Enforcement is by ticket checkers (TCs and travelling ticket examiners) at station exits, on trains and in planned "station checks, ambush checks, fortress checks, intensive inspections and mega drives", with frequent checks on AC trains ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-local-train-crackdown-railways-recover-over-188-crore-in-fines-from-ticketless-ac-commuters)). They scan phone tickets on a **handheld terminal** that shows whether a QR is genuine, and Western Railway's Mumbai Central division has piloted **15 body cameras** on checkers, mainly in AC trains ([Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-local-train-two-arrested-for-travelling-in-first-class-with-fake-uts-passes-a522); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/no-more-he-said-she-said-western-railway-to-record-tc-passenger-encounters-using-body-cameras)). Under the **Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2026**, in force from 1 July 2026 (some Mumbai reports say 19–20 June), the minimum penalty for travelling without a valid ticket doubled from **₹250 to ₹500, plus the fare**; using someone else's ticket or entering restricted railway areas costs ₹500, a **man in a women's coach pays ₹2,500**, carrying hazardous goods at least ₹10,000, and these civil penalties rise 10 % every three years, with court only if the penalty goes unpaid ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/railways-rule-change-from-july-1-travelling-without-a-ticket-how-much-fine-youll-have-to-pay-538856-2026-06-24); [Business Today, 30 June](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/indian-railways-hikes-penalties-for-ticketless-travel-womens-coach-entry-from-july-540123-2026-06-30); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/news/indian-railways-raises-penalties-for-ticketless-travel-and-other-offences-from-july-2026)). Before July the rule was the fare plus the larger of the fare or ₹250, and the average AC case cost about ₹311.

Enforcement in 2026 is heavy: Central Railway's Mumbai division logged **4.30 lakh ticketless or irregular cases and ₹30.13 crore** (1 crore = 10 million) in April–May 2026 and 9,509 cases in a single day on 18 April; Western Railway collected ₹100.42 crore in April–August 2026 and catches about **330 ticketless riders a day in AC trains** ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/central-railway-recovers-30-crore-from-ticketless-travellers-in-just-two-months-mumbai-division-detects-43-lakh-cases); [Free Press Journal, one day](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/central-railway-catches-9509-ticketless-passengers-in-mumbai-division-collects-7138-lakh-fine-in-a-day); [Free Press Journal, WR](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/western-railway-collects-100-crore-in-fines-from-ticketless-travellers-in-five-months-ac-local-violations-rise-172); [Free Press Journal, AC](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-news-ac-local-trains-see-spike-in-ticketless-travel-as-western-railway-catches-330-daily)). Forged tickets, from edited screenshots to fake apps and AI-made passes, lead to police cases and custody ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-news-3-passengers-booked-for-using-ai-generated-fake-uts-season-tickets-on-ac-local)). Even so, against millions of daily rides the real chance of being checked on one ride is well under 1 %.

### Metro tickets: gates, time limits and ₹10 steps

The metro is the opposite: every station has security (X-ray baggage scanners and frisking) and flap gates. Every line charges by distance in ₹10 steps; **Line 3 (Aqua Line) charges ₹10 to 3 km, ₹20 to 12 km, ₹30 to 18 km, ₹40 to 24 km, ₹50 to 30 km and ₹60 to 36 km**, so Cuffe Parade–CSMT costs ₹20 and the whole line ₹60, while Line 1 charges ₹10–40 ([Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/transport/mumbai-metro-3-travel-from-aarey-to-cuffe-parade-in-60-nariman-point-to-airport-in-50-using-aqua-line-90050); [Housing.com](https://housing.com/news/mumbai-metro-line-1-route-stations-maps/)). On Line 1 a passenger who exits at the entry station must do so **within 20 minutes**, others have 75 minutes, overstaying costs **₹10 an hour up to ₹50**, and tickets expire at the end of the day ([Reliance Mumbai Metro FAQ](https://www.reliancemumbaimetro.com/metro-faq)); on Lines 2A and 7 a paper single-journey ticket must be used within 60 minutes, and a rider who goes past the paid fare pays the difference at a customer-care desk by the exit gate ([MMMOCL fare media](https://www.mmmocl.co.in/fare_media); [MMMOCL QR terms](https://www.mmmocl.co.in/terms-conditions-for-qr-ticket.html)). Line 1 gates handle about **35 people a minute each** ([Indian Infrastructure](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2026/03/06/digital-ticketing-building-a-future-ready-afc-system-for-mumbai-metro-line-1/)). Line 3 sells National Common Mobility Card (NCMC) tourist passes at **₹280 for one day and ₹700 for three**, and a 25 % student concession since 2026 ([Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-metro-line-3-introduces-discounted-passes-for-students-and-tourists-check-rates-a525); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-3-mmrc-extends-25-per-cent-student-fare-concession-to-graduation-level-students-on-aqua-line)). Eating inside metro coaches is banned, and Line 3's underground stations lost mobile signal for months over a dispute with telecom companies, which stranded phone payments ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/no-civic-sense-man-seen-eating-inside-mumbai-metro-3-sparks-debate-online-video); [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-metro-line-3-network-blackout-leaves-passengers-unable-to-make-upi-payments-or-calls-a525)). The price gap is real: an Andheri–Churchgate AC local pass costs about ₹1,335 a month against about ₹3,500 on Line 3 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/pay-extra-for-peaceful-travel-netizens-react-as-commuter-compares-rates-between-ac-local-train-vs-mumbai-metro-3)). Metro trains run from about **5:30 to 23:50 on Line 1 (every 3 min 20 s at peak) and 5:55 to 22:30 departures on Line 3 (every 4 min 25 s, 337 weekday trips)** ([Mumbai Metro Times](https://www.mumbaimetrotimes.com/mumbai-metro-timings-line-1/); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-line-3-adds-46-weekday-trips-aqua-line-services-rise-to-337-as-peak-hour-waiting-time-drops)), and the Monorail has carried **no passengers since 20 September 2025**, with no restart reported by mid-November 2026 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/mmrda-temporarily-halts-mumbai-monorail-for-upgrades-fleet-overhaul-125092000131_1.html)).

## Twenty-metre coaches stop for twenty seconds between 400-metre signals

### The rakes

A Mumbai suburban coach is about **20.726 m long, 3.658 m wide and 3.810 m high**, much wider than an ordinary Indian coach (about 3.25 m), and trains are built from three-coach units of one motor coach and two trailers, so a 12-car train is four units ([Slideshare EMU overview](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/comprehensive-overview-of-emu-and-memu-train-systems-in-india/288087346); [BEML catalogue](https://alumni.bemlindia.in/writereaddata/Downloads/2017082510404804cat_SS%20EMU.docx); [IRIMEE "Basics of EMU"](https://rskr.irimee.in/sites/default/files/EMU%20BASICS.pdf)). Over the bodies a 12-car train is therefore about **249 m and a 15-car about 311 m**. The fleet is built at the Integral Coach Factory in Chennai with different traction equipment: 133 Siemens-equipped rakes from the first Mumbai Urban Transport Project (MUTP), common on Central and Harbour lines, 72 Bombardier-equipped rakes from the second, and Medha and Bharat Heavy Electricals (BHEL) rakes, including the AC trains ([Wikipedia mirror, Mumbai Suburban Railway](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Mumbai_local); [Hindustan Times via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-st-mumbai/20180919/281715500527512)). Top speed is about **100 km/h for Siemens-type and 105–110 km/h for Bombardier and Medha-type rakes**, and no reliable acceleration figure was found; 0.5–0.8 m/s² comes from a low-quality comparison ([Traffic Infra Tech](https://trafficinfratech.com/?p=11811); [The Week, 18-car trials](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2026/01/06/western-railway-to-soon-conduct-trials-of-18-coach-local-trains.html)). An AC rake seats fewer, about 1,028, with roof-mounted air-conditioning units and automatic doors ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/headlines/ac-rake-arrives-in-mumbai-but-cool-commute-later)). After commuters fell from two crowded trains near Mumbra in June 2025, the Railway Ministry decided all new trains get automatic doors and older non-AC coaches will be retrofitted; the **first non-AC rake with automatic doors**, with louvred panels so air flows when they are shut, reached Kurla car shed on 13 April 2026 for trials ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/indian-railways-to-equip-all-new-local-trains-with-automatic-doors-non-ac-coaches-to-be-retrofitted); [Free Press Journal, first look](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/first-look-mumbai-gets-non-ac-local-train-with-automatic-doors-new-emu-arrives-at-kurla-shed-video)). Further out, 238 "Vande Metro"-type AC rakes are on order with the first due in 2027, and 18-car trials were planned on Virar–Dahanu Road in January 2026 with no start date for service ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/tired-of-crowded-mumbai-locals-238-new-ac-trains-promise-cooler-safer-travel-from-2027-548980-2026-08-13); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/rdso-to-conduct-emergency-braking-distance-and-coupler-force-trials-for-18-car-ac-emu-rakes-on-western-railway-network)).

### Coach order, liveries and doors

Coach positions are **fixed per line and painted on every platform** (boards for first class, women and the disabled; first-class stops marked by red pillars with yellow or white stripes), though the boards do not say whether a position is for a 12-car or a 15-car train ([OpenStreetMap community forum](https://community.openstreetmap.org/t/coach-positions-for-mumbai-suburban-railways/130878); [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/?p=39177); [Outlook Traveller](https://www.outlookindia.com/traveller/destinations/a-dummys-guide-to-the-mumbai-locals/)). No primary coach diagram was found, so the order is only partly known. Western Railway's ladies compartments sit **towards the Virar end**: in October 2022 it added a ladies section in the **11th coach of 12 (14th of 15) from Churchgate** per one report, or in the 10th from Churchgate per another ([LatestLY](https://www.latestly.com/agency-news/india-news-mumbai-wr-adds-25-seats-for-women-in-all-suburban-local-trains-4317327.html); [Mid-day](https://midday-re.vercel.app/mumbai/mumbai-news/article/mumbai-extra-womens-compartment-added-to-all-wr-non-ac-locals-23249967)), and it planned to turn the 7th coach from Churchgate into a senior-citizens' section ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/western-railway-rolls-out-senior-citizen-coaches-in-27-locals-central-railway-lags-behind-with-just-five)). On Central Railway the **luggage compartment is in the 6th coach from the CSMT end** and has been converted into a 13-seat senior-citizens' compartment since July 2025, with the remaining 163 trains due by September 2026 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/central-railway-unveils-mumbais-first-dedicated-local-train-compartment-for-senior-citizens-western-railway-to-follow-soon)); a Central 12-car has about 1,161 seats, 245 of them for women, and four general first-class compartments ([DNA India](https://dnaindia.com/mumbai/report-need-for-more-ladies-coaches-but-officials-undecided-2003568); [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/maharashtra/mumbai-railway-to-create-separate-compartment-for-senior-citizens-in-local-train-a505/amp)).

Liveries are documented only in parts: **red stripes over a grey or steel body mark first class and green or lime marks ladies coaches** (Western Railway's ladies coaches once carried a painted smiling woman), the first AC rake was **silver and blue**, Central Railway painted its AC cab fronts **yellow** so track workers could see them, and one AC local wore cricket-team colours for the 2026 Indian Premier League ([CurlyTales](https://curlytales.com/why-do-indian-train-coaches-have-yellow-and-red-colour-stripes-the-reason-is-surprising/); [Asian Age](https://www.asianage.com/life/more-features/270519/western-railway-embraces-the-modern-woman-avatar.html); [DeshGujarat](https://deshgujarat.com/2016/04/05/mumbai-gets-its-first-ever-ac-local-trial-run-within-a-week/); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-central-railway-paints-faces-of-ac-local-rakes-yellow-to-curb-visibility-issues); [Free Press Journal, IPL wrap](https://www.freepressjournal.in/lifestyle/ac-local-train-transforms-into-mumbai-indians-colours-for-ipl-2026-commuters-cant-get-enough-of-it)). Whole-train base colours by railway were not confirmed by any source, nor were the number and width of doors per coach side; ordinary coaches run with open doorways, which is why doorway crowds and door-hangers define the look of a non-AC peak train.

### Stops, signals, indicators and announcements

An ordinary local stops for **"nearly 20 seconds"** at each station, while AC trains stop close to **a minute** because of their door cycles, which Western Railway asked the builder to speed up; door delays, alarm chains and trespassers can push delays to 20–25 minutes on a bad day ([TogetherVCan, citing Hindustan Times](https://togethervcan.in/?p=2616); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/western-railway-services-delayed-up-to-25-minutes-on-first-day-of-revised-timetable)). Signalling is automatic block with signals **about 400–500 m apart** showing red, yellow (next signal red), double yellow and green, and an onboard Auxiliary Warning System that keeps warning the motorman until a better aspect appears ([Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/business/3903622-mumbai-local-trains-get-upgraded-signal-system-for-error-free-operations); [IRFCA](https://irfca.org/docs/mumbai-aws.html)). Platform indicators traditionally show a short destination code, the time, "F" or "S" for fast or slow and the minutes to arrival, as in **"Bo 8:42 F 03"** (Borivali, 8:42, fast, due in 3 minutes); Central and Harbour codes include **T Thane, K Kalyan, A Ambernath, S Karjat, N Kasara, ST for CSMT, PL Panvel, BR Belapur, VA Vashi and M Mankhurd**, though light-emitting diode (LED) boards increasingly spell names out and Western Railway's own platform abbreviations were not verified ([Mumbai Trains blog](https://mumbai-trains.blogspot.com/2009/12/reading-indicators-at-mumbai-local.html?m=1); [Mumbai77](https://www.mumbai77.com/city/7062/travel/mumbai-railway-stations-short-codes/); [IRFCA FAQ](https://irfca.org/faq/faq-station.html)). Since 2024 Western Railway coaches carry **side displays** showing the destination in English, Hindi and Marathi (switching every 3 seconds), fast or slow, and 12 or 15 cars ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2024/06/13/bes11-mh-train-display.html)). Train numbers have five digits starting with 9, odd away from town and even towards it: on the Western line 90xxx ordinary, 91xxx late night, 92xxx mostly 15-car, 93xxx Dahanu Road and 94xxx AC; on Central, the pattern seen in listings is 95xxx fast and through, 96xxx slow beyond Kalyan, 97xxx CSMT–Kalyan slow, 98xxx Harbour and 99xxx Trans-Harbour, with letter codes such as K17, BL11 and S5 ([2023–24 timetable](https://github.com/Umang-Lodaya/Mumbai-Local-TimeTable-Extractor/tree/ba5005759da90af0fbd8b8988f9863b1629fef11/data); [IRFCA FAQ](https://irfca.org/faq/faq-number.html); [indiarailinfo](https://indiarailinfo.com/train/timetable/k17-mumbai-csmt-kalyan-slow-local-97027/20592/12282/72)). On-board announcements usually come in **Marathi, Hindi and English** in one recorded female voice belonging to a real person, so the game needs its own voice; the exact wording, which names the next station and the side the doors open, was not found in a source ([Transparent Language blog](https://blogs.transparent.com/hindi/?p=7975); [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/133145/meet-sarla-chaudhary-the-woman-who-has-been-making-all-the-local-mumbai-train-announcements)). No source described the trains' sounds: horn, motor whine, door chimes or rail clatter all need recordings.

Level crossings are now rare inside Mumbai; recent news covers only fringe gates such as Titwala–Khadavli and Diva, which road bridges are replacing ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-new-four-lane-road-over-bridge-between-titwala-khadavli-to-open-soon)), so the game's level crossings in the island city are a creative choice rather than a match for reality.

## South Mumbai's 27 local stations, measured and described

### What every station shares

After a 2014 Bombay High Court direction, suburban platforms were raised from 760–840 mm to the **840–920 mm band, with 900 mm called optimal**, and Western Railway says it raised all 166 of its platforms ([Lok Sabha Committee on Petitions](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/65556/1/16_Petitions_36.pdf); [TogetherVCan](https://togethervcan.in/?p=1852)). Central Railway lengthened busy platforms **from 270 m to 330 m** for 15-car trains, and the 15-car extension programme (34 platforms) is still running ([Central line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_line_(Mumbai_Suburban_Railway)); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/34-mumbai-railway-platforms-to-be-lengthened-for-15-coach-trains-as-part-of-33000-crore-urban-transport-upgrade)). Station name boards are **black text on yellow** because it reads best at a distance and in the dark, stacked in three languages; a 2024 trial of tricolour square boards was scrapped within days ([Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/society/railway-station-names-written-on-yellow-board-visible-in-dark-76562); [Mid-day](https://midday-re.vercel.app/mumbai/mumbai-news/article/mumbai-tricolour-signboards-scrapped-23357843)). Footbridges come in a few widths: older and Army-built ones about **3.5–3.65 m**, and the 2018–2026 generation **6, 8, 10 or 12 m** wide with stainless-steel railings and lifts; the Western line had 102 footbridges and 8 subways in 2013 ([Bharatpedia, Western line](https://en.bharatpedia.org/wiki/Western_Line_(Mumbai_Suburban_Railway)); [Free Press Journal, Grant Road](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-western-railway-opens-new-10-metre-wide-fob-at-grant-road-station)). The Amrit Bharat Station Scheme (a national station-upgrade programme) has given most island-city stations a facelift since 2023 — new booking offices, granite or tiled platforms, new platform covers, trilingual smart indicators (installed on Western stations in July 2025) and new footbridges — leaving heritage fabric at Byculla and Reay Road ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-news-western-railway-installs-smart-multilingual-train-indicators-at-key-suburban-stations-under-amrit-bharat-scheme); [Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/civic/chinchpokli-matunga-parel-vadala-road-and-shahad-stations-get-major-upgrades-91061)). Stalls stand on the platforms themselves. Canopy types, column spacing, benches, water coolers and clocks are not described in any source found.

### Western stations, Churchgate to Mahim

**Churchgate** is a four-platform dead-end terminus, platforms 1–2 for slow and 3–4 for fast trains, extended for 15-car trains in 2010, under a **seven-storey office block opened on 3 June 1957** that houses the Western Railway headquarters; the heritage building across the road is the old Bombay, Baroda and Central India Railway headquarters in Venetian Gothic and Indo-Saracenic style ([TIFR Bombay architecture](https://theory.tifr.res.in/bombay/architecture/building/churchgate.html); [FIBIS](https://wiki.fibis.org/w/Bombay_Churchgate_HQ_and_Station_BB&CIR); [Churchgate railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchgate_railway_station)). The mapped outlines put narrow side strips (3.5–4.8 m) outside the outer tracks and 7.3–7.4 m islands between them, plus a **112 m pedestrian subway under all four tracks**; Metro Line 3's Churchgate station is 444 m away ([osm-stations.json](../osm-stations.json)). A plan to clad the block in tinted glass was objected to and never confirmed as built ([DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/mumbai/report-churchgate-to-get-a-touch-of-glass-1485859)). **Marine Lines** is getting a **12 m-wide footbridge**, whose 19 steel girders were launched overnight on 30–31 May 2026 ([PropNewsTime](https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MzExNTU=/marine-lines-station-foot-overbridge-work-advances-with-launch-of-19-steel-girders-during-major-rail-block)). **Charni Road** (opened 1868) has four platforms and three footbridges, including a skywalk-style bridge running parallel to the platform, and escalators were planned in February 2026 ([Rail Analysis](https://news.railanalysis.com/western-railway-commissions-new-foot-over-bridge-fob-at-charni-road-station/); [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/india/charni-road-station-footover-bridge-collapse); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-central-grant-road-charni-road-stations-to-get-escalators-beautification)). **Grant Road**, the railway's first terminus in 1867, has a December 1969 building and a 10 m footbridge opened in 2020 ([Grant Road railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Road_railway_station)).

**Mumbai Central** was designed by **Claude Batley** and built by Shapoorji Pallonji in a record 21 months, opening on **18 December 1930**; sources dispute whether its style is Art Deco or Gothic, so photographs should decide ([mumbaimetroroute heritage page](https://mumbaimetroroute.com/places/mumbai-central-station-heritage); [FIBIS](https://wiki.fibis.org/w/Bombay_Central_Station_BB&CIR)). Its **five main-line platforms end at a large concourse at the south end** (mapped as a 49.5 × 118 m covered area) beside **four suburban platforms**, all linked by footbridges, and a temporary footbridge serves the station while the Bellasis Road bridge is rebuilt ([Wanderlog](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/3903730/mumbai-central-railway-station-building); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/western-railway-mcgm-open-temporary-fob-at-mumbai-central-amid-bellasis-road-over-bridge-reconstruction)). **Mahalaxmi** is a single island on the slow lines; a **travelator skywalk**, Mumbai's first, was nearing completion in July 2026 to link the Monorail's Sant Gadge Maharaj Chowk station with Metro Line 3 and the railway station, though its final length (384 m or about 200 m) is unresolved ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbais-first-travelator-skywalk-takes-shape-to-link-metro-line-3-with-monorail-at-mahalaxmi); [Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/amp/en/infrastructure/mumbais-first-travelator-skywalk-connecting-mumbai-monorail-and-mahalakshmi-metro-station-shortened-92869)). **Lower Parel** has three platforms on five tracks and new smart indicators ([Lower Parel railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Parel_railway_station)).

**Prabhadevi** (renamed from Elphinstone Road) and Central Railway's **Parel** form one complex. The Army's Bombay Sappers built a **73.1 m × 3.65 m steel footbridge** between them, opened on 27 February 2018 ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/3-fobs-built-by-indian-army-inaugurated-for-mumbai-rail-commuters/53226.html)). The 1913-era **Elphinstone road bridge** above both stations, which held two of Prabhadevi's exits, closed in September 2025 and was demolished by spring 2026; a **double-decker road bridge with open-web steel girders** (part of the 4.5 km Sewri–Worli elevated corridor) is being built in its place, due late 2026 or 2027 ([India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/maharashtra/mumbai-elphinstone-bridge-closed-for-demolition-to-make-way-for-new-double-decker-connector-historic-125-years-old-structure-shut-2025-09-13-1007987); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/mumbais-elphinstone-bridge-becomes-history-new-east-west-connector-to-rise/2897251)). In November 2026 this is a construction site, a ready-made set piece. Prabhadevi's South footbridge is being widened with an extra staircase ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/western-railway-starts-prabhadevi-fob-expansion-work-additional-staircase-planned-to-improve-passenger-movement)). Parel became a **suburban terminus on 3–4 March 2019**, with a terminating line, a double-discharge platform and a **12 m east–west footbridge**; its 2025 facelift may be torn down for the CSMT–Kurla 5th and 6th lines and a new long-distance Parel terminus approved in late 2025 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/parel-suburban-terminus-inaugurated-119030300609_1.html); [RailPost](https://www.railpost.in/?p=1536); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbais-parel-railway-stations-19-crore-amrit-bharat-upgrade-may-be-demolished-within-a-year-for-new-rail-lines-terminus)).

**Dadar** is two stations under one name, 207 m apart and joined by shared footbridges. Since **9 December 2023** they use one numbering: **Western platforms 1–7, Central 8–14** (old Central 3 became 9, 4 became 10 and so on, and old Central 2 was closed to widen platform 1), with Central 13–14 forming the long-distance "Dadar Terminus" ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/india-news/new-platform-numbering-at-dadar-junction-from-dec-9-to-lessen-confusion-123092701539_1.html)). A Western Railway official counted **eight footbridges**; the newest Western one is **8 m wide and 58 m long** at the south end, the Central slow platform for Kalyan-bound trains was widened from **7 m to 10.5 m**, platform 10 is becoming double-discharge with 11, elevated decks linking the footbridges were speeding up in April 2026, and a new Western long-distance platform "7A" is planned ([Free Press Journal, FOB](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-new-western-railway-foot-over-bridge-comes-up-at-dadar-station); [Free Press Journal, widening](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/mumbai-central-railways-dadar-station-widening-project-nears-completion-move-to-enhance-commuter-experience); [Free Press Journal, decks](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/dadar-railway-station-upgrade-gains-speed-elevated-decks-fob-links-to-ease-23-lakh-daily-passenger-rush)). OpenStreetMap tags one shared footbridge at 12 m and maps 7 entrances, 2 lifts and 30 ticket points on the Western side. **Mahim Junction** has six platforms (Harbour on 5/6) and reopened its South footbridge rebuilt in corrosion-resistant stainless steel in 2025 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-news-western-railway-reopens-mahim-station-south-foot-over-bridge-with-corrosion-resistant-stainless-steel-for-commuter-safety)).

### Central and Harbour stations, CSMT to Sion and King's Circle

**CSMT** has **18 platforms: 1–7 suburban (Harbour 1–2, slow 3–4, fast 5–6) and 8–18 long-distance** ([Deccan Herald](https://deccanherald.com/india/maharashtra/csmt-can-now-accommodate-24-coach-trains-3048778); [OpenStreetMap 8130328](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/8130328)). The building is F. W. Stevens's **Victorian Gothic Revival terminus (1878–1888)** with Italian Gothic and Indian elements, an **octagonal ribbed dome** whose eight stone ribs carry the **statue of Progress, about 14 ft (4.3 m) tall**, and UNESCO World Heritage status since 2004 ([Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/cQUBqFzBGzp0Jw); [Victorian Web](https://victorianweb.org/victorian/art/architecture/stevens/7.html); [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/document/154660)). OpenStreetMap maps 24 roof pieces, including gabled roofs over suburban platforms 1–7, and the heritage block at 7 levels. A redevelopment with an **elevated concourse over platforms 8–18** is under way on the P. D'Mello Road side, while the suburban shed and the Stevens front stay the postcard view; two conflicting plans would either lift the Harbour line onto an elevated corridor or end it at Sandhurst Road, and neither is confirmed for 2026 ([Construction World](https://www.constructionworld.in/gold/transport-infrastructure/metro-rail-and-railways-infrastructure/csmt-redevelopment-project-has-received-approval-from-the-heritage-committee/33454); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-harbour-line-set-to-change-track)). The game must show this station as **Chhatrapati Chai Terminus**. **Masjid** has four platforms on six tracks, two Harbour and two main line, with no fast stop ([Masjid railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masjid_railway_station)).

**Sandhurst Road** is billed as **India's first two-tier station**: two Central platforms at ground level and two Harbour platforms on a **527 m (1,728 ft) steel viaduct** opened in February 1925, with the elevated Harbour line continuing towards Sewri; OpenStreetMap tags the Harbour tracks `bridge=viaduct, layer=1` with both Harbour platforms on the upper level ([Sandhurst Road railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandhurst_Road_railway_station); [osm-stations.json](../osm-stations.json)). A 2017 plan to demolish it for the 5th and 6th lines had not been carried out. **Byculla** is the heritage gem: commissioned in 1853, its **stone building of 1887–1891** is Victorian Gothic and **Grade IIA**, with verandah roofs on **cast-iron posts set on basalt walls**, carved wooden brackets, cast-iron railings, stained glass and timber panelling, and station signs now hung as **plain teak planks from recreated cast-iron brackets**; the 2021–22 restoration won a **UNESCO Asia-Pacific Award of Merit** ([HUDCO Design Awards p.69](https://hudco.org.in/writereaddata/EB/HUDCO-DA23/files/basic-html/page69.html); [Global Indian](https://globalindian.com/didyouknow/byculla-one-of-the-oldest-indian-railway-stations-with-gothic-style-of-architecture-has-received-the-unescos-asia-pacific-cultural-heritage-preservation-award-of-merit-for-restoration-work)). **Chinchpokli** (1867) and **Currey Road** (1884) got new buildings in 1893–95 and are single islands on the slow lines; Currey Road's second footbridge was tendered at 30 m × 3.5 m ([IRFCA timeline](https://irfca.org/docs/mumbai-cr-suburban-timeline.html); [Hindustan Times via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-st-mumbai/20140821/281891591443205)). **Matunga** (1883, four platforms) has had an **all-women staff since 2017**, and **Sion** (1873, four platforms) had its road bridge rebuilt in 2024–25 for the new lines ([Matunga railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matunga_railway_station); [Time Out Mumbai](https://www.timeout.com/mumbai/news/mumbais-sion-railway-overbridge-may-reopen-this-weekend-but-theres-a-catch-101526)).

On the Harbour line, **Reay Road** (1910, Grade I) is a **single-storey stone station with hooded segmental arches, rectangular pilasters and a lean-to roof**, the best-described small station for hand-building ([Reay Road railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reay_Road_railway_station)); **Dockyard Road** (12 November 1925) was built by cutting away part of Mazagaon Hill, though another extract calls it elevated, so its level should come from OpenStreetMap's bridge and layer tags; **Cotton Green** faces the **Art Deco Cotton Exchange of 1924**; **Vadala Road** (1914) is a four-platform junction whose renovated boards started a "Wadala or Vadala" spelling debate; and **King's Circle** is an elevated station next to Matunga ([Harbour line](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_Line); [Cotton Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_Green); [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/wadala-or-vadala-name-confusion-at-renovated-mumbai-station-sparks-online-debate-central-railway-responds-a525/amp); [King's Circle railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King%27s_Circle_railway_station)). Dockyard Road and Cotton Green have no crossing footbridge mapped, so the game must place one by hand. The new elevated Kurla Harbour station, part of the 5th and 6th line project, had not opened by the latest reports ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-railway-update-kurla-to-get-elevated-harbour-line-station-as-5th-and-6th-line-project-picks-up-pace)).

Eight British-era names have state approval to change (**Currey Road → Lalbaug, Sandhurst Road → Dongri, Marine Lines → Mumbadevi, Charni Road → Girgaon, Cotton Green → Kalachowki, King's Circle → Tirthankar Parshvanath**, Mumbai Central and others), but no Union government approval was found, so the boards most likely still show the old names; only Elphinstone Road → Prabhadevi has taken effect ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2024/03/12/bes14-mh-station-names.html); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/maharashtra/maharashtra-council-passes-resolution-to-rename-seven-local-train-stations-3098254)).

### Long-distance terminals

Besides CSMT's platforms 8–18 and Mumbai Central's five, Dadar Terminus is Central platforms 13–14, **Lokmanya Tilak Terminus** at Kurla has five (or seven) platforms with two more nearing completion and about ₹5,000 crore of upgrades announced, and **Bandra Terminus** has seven mapped platform numbers; their longest mapped platforms run **546–655 m** ([totaltraininfo](https://www.totaltraininfo.com/station/ltt/8); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-lokmanya-tilak-terminus-expansion-nears-completion-2-new-platforms-to-cater-to-summer-rush-by-march); [osm-stations.json](../osm-stations.json)).

## Metro boxes lie 18–24 metres down, monorail halts six metres up

### Line 3 underground stations

No station-by-station depth table was found. Of Line 3's underground stations, 19 or 20 are cut-and-cover boxes **"normally up to 250 m long × 22–30 m deep"**, and seven in congested areas (**Hutatma Chowk, Kalbadevi, Girgaon, Grant Road, Shitladevi, Santacruz, Marol Naka**) combine cut-and-cover with caverns mined by the New Austrian Tunnelling Method (NATM); running tunnels lie on average **15–25 m below the street** with a **5.8 m finished inside diameter**, and the Cuffe Parade and Bandra Kurla Complex (BKC) boxes reach **450 m** with their crossovers ([TunnelTalk](https://www.tunneltalk.com/India-12Mar2020-Mumbai-Metro-Line-3-excavation-methods-advance-and-statistics.php); [SYSTRA](https://www.systra.com/india/project/mumbai-metro-line-3/); [CICI](https://www.cici.co.in/index.php/mrts/111-mumbai-metro-mm3-cbs-ugc-04); [Indian Infrastructure](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2026/07/03/tunnelling-through-complexity-mmrcls-experience-in-executing-indias-first-fully-underground-metro-corridor/)). The only per-station depth found is the airport's Terminal 2, 26.5 m or about 30 m and the deepest. The stations are two-level (street, concourse, platform), mostly with one island platform for 8-car trains; the ten Line 3 platforms mapped in OpenStreetMap (all north of Dharavi) measure **178–186 m**, and the 8-car Alstom train is 177.2 m ([Cuffe Parade metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuffe_Parade_metro_station); [osm-metro.json](../osm-metro.json); [International Railway Journal](https://www.railjournal.com/passenger/metros/mumbai-metro-line-3-train-design-revealed/)). Two stations break the pattern: **Kalbadevi** is a mined three-vault station, two **9.45 m platform tunnels** with a central circulation cavern between them, ten cross passages and two small end boxes, so it has two separate platforms rather than an island; **Shitladevi** is a 240 m station with one track in a box and the other in a mined tunnel ([ResearchGate, Kalbadevi paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370548457_Construction_of_Kalbadevi_underground_metro_station_of_Mumbai_metro_line_3_by_dealing_with_numerous_social_and_technical_challenges_in_the_congested_old_city_-_financial_capital_of_India); [The Metro Rail Guy](https://themetrorailguy.com/2021/04/26/mumbai-metro-records-natm-breakthrough-at-shitladevi-station/)). OpenStreetMap maps the Dadar box at 346 × 41 m.

Entrances are lettered by concourse end (A1, A2… and B1, B2…) and open in stages; where both ends are mapped they lie **140–350 m apart**, so passages reach out to street corners, and counts run from one or two mapped (Girgaon, Kalbadevi) to **seven at Siddhivinayak** ([MumbaiMetro3 on X](https://x.com/MumbaiMetro3/status/1922922494325031398); [osm-metro.json](../osm-metro.json)). Most are glass-and-steel kiosks over stairs and escalators with separate lift boxes. **Hutatma Chowk's entrances are roofless stone-and-glass wells**, with only lift shafts and some ventilation structures above ground, plus a two-storey entry styled after the neighbouring Commissariat Building with a police post upstairs; **CSMT's entrance is a curved glass arched or domed pavilion** framing the terminus, with a lattice roof and heritage murals inside ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-3-aqua-line-why-hutatma-chowk-station-has-open-to-sky-entrances-mmrc-clarifies); [Urban Acres](https://urbanacres.in/mumbai-metro-3-integrates-heritage-with-modern-design-at-flora-fountain); [Curly Tales](https://curlytales.com/india/travel/mumbai-metro-aqua-line-csmt-station-nears-completion-with-arched-entrance-glass-design-modern-facade/)). Inside, Line 3 has Mumbai's first **full-height glass platform screen doors**, wide concourses, polished granite floors and walls, high ceilings, large escalators, tactile paths and trilingual signs, with name boards in Marathi and English ([YouTube, MMRC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0a6QSTvg3r8); [Free Press Journal, Kalbadevi](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-3-first-glimpses-of-kalbadevi-station-on-aqua-line-unveiled-check-out-visuals)). In South Mumbai, Line 3 runs under the Western line's stations, so one underground interchange pattern repeats: Grant Road 195 m, Mumbai Central (Jagannath Shankar Sheth) 201 m, Mahalaxmi 336 m, Marine Lines to Kalbadevi 371 m, CSMT 415 m, Charni Road to Girgaon 419 m and Churchgate 442 m apart ([osm-stations.json `metroNearby`](../osm-stations.json)).

### Monorail southern stations

Monorail stations are **compact two-storey elevated boxes**, a concourse over the road and platforms above, at a minimum height of **about 5.5 m**, with four escalators each (two street to concourse, two concourse to platform), lifts and ticket counters ([Mumbai Monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Monorail); [MMMOCL station facilities](https://www.mmmocl.co.in/station-facilities.html)). The southern stations mapped in OpenStreetMap measure **68–69 m × 18–22 m** (Acharya Atre Nagar, Antop Hill, Guru Tegh Bahadur Nagar, Wadala Bridge), with two side platforms about **60 m long** outside the two beams (Antop Hill); phase-1 stations further east are longer, 74–77 m ([osm-metro.json](../osm-metro.json)). Footprints for Dadar East, Naigaon, Ambedkar Nagar, Mint Colony, Lower Parel and Sant Gadge Maharaj Chowk are not mapped. The line was built and safety-certified but not carrying passengers in November 2026. The future underground **Line 11** (Wadala to the Gateway of India, 14 stations) was still awaiting central approval in September 2026, so nothing of it exists on the ground ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-line-11-awaits-centres-nod-nearly-a-year-after-state-approval-wadala-gateway-corridor-still-pending)).

## What the game should build, use and rename

### The data files already hold the stations

[osm-stations.json](../osm-stations.json) (1.67 MB), written by [scripts/research/stations-from-osm.mjs](../../../../scripts/research/stations-from-osm.mjs) in about 7 seconds (`node scripts/research/stations-from-osm.mjs`), covers **43 stations in the map box, 27 of them in South Mumbai**, with names (including `name:mr` for all but ten), codes, lines from the 64 route relations, the frame centre and axis, **130 platforms** (outlines in game metres and latitude/longitude, inferred length and width, numbered edges, and the tracks along each edge with their services), middle cross-sections, clipped track centrelines, **100 footbridge networks with 124 spans over the tracks** (ways, landings that touch platforms, steps, 31 escalator ways and 18 lifts), **145 buildings of which 66 are roofs or platform structures**, 31 entrances at 13 stations, 94 ticket points, nearby metro stations, a `game` block of the stops the current train code uses, and a list of gaps and data issues per station. Its weaknesses are as clear as its strengths: **no platform, footbridge or roof heights anywhere**, footbridge widths tagged on only 9 networks (the file's 4 m is a placeholder), no roof mapped at 23 stations, no entrance at 30, and three platforms tagged only `public_transport=platform` that the converter currently ignores (Andheri 8/9, Khar Road 1A, Lokmanya Tilak Terminus 6/7). [osm-metro.json](../osm-metro.json) (0.78 MB, from `scripts/research/metro-from-osm.mjs`) holds every metro and monorail line's centrelines, stops, 119 platform outlines and 82 entrances, but no heights and no Line 3 platforms south of BKC.

Today the converter draws platforms as flat paving 0.21 m high, footbridges as flat 2.2 m ribbons at kerb height across the tracks, and the Sandhurst Road Harbour viaduct at ground level, while it raises buildings mapped over the tracks to a **6.2 m clearance** ([scripts/build-tiles.mjs](../../../../scripts/build-tiles.mjs); [src/world/mesh/ground.js](../../../../src/world/mesh/ground.js)). The train simulation already uses a 21 m coach pitch (252 m for 12 cars), a 20-second dwell, a 30-second terminus turn, a 150-second peak interval per track and an approximate coach order with Western ladies coaches towards the Virar end and Central's luggage or senior coach sixth from CSMT ([src/sim/trains.js](../../../../src/sim/trains.js)), all of which match the evidence above. The station work is therefore mostly vertical: lift each platform outline to platform height, put footbridge decks (`footbridges[].ways[].line`) at deck height with stairs where `ends` fall on platforms, drape the roof buildings over the platforms, and raise the Harbour viaduct at Sandhurst Road and wherever else OpenStreetMap tags the Harbour tracks as bridges.

### Names that must change, and content to keep out

The project's rules forbid real brands, logos and apps on screen while keeping real places, and the railway world is full of them. OpenStreetMap stores metro sponsor names in `official_name`, so the converter should read only `name` and `name:mr`.

Content rules bind the stations as much as the trains: **no reference to the 2006 train bombings, the 2008 attack (which struck CSMT), riots or gangsters**; the 2017 Elphinstone crush appears only as the brief safety fact above, never as a scene; the 2025 Mumbra falls stay out of game content; hawkers, beggars and transgender people are shown with respect; and nothing mocks poverty at any station. The harmless real-world gags are plentiful: the geo-fence that refuses to sell a ticket "inside station premises", the saved-QR trick that works until a checker reads the booking time, fares that "haven't gone up since 2022", a body-camera checker, the luggage-compartment dabbawalas, the bhajan group with cymbals, the "fourth seat", and Sunday announcements that the slow train will run on the fast line, 15 minutes late.

### Open gaps

Several things the owner will want were not found and should stay configurable: a **primary coach-order diagram** for any line in 2026 (and whether AC rakes follow it); the **number and width of doors per coach side, the coach floor height** and so the platform gap; **canopy types, roof and footbridge heights**, column spacing and furniture at any station; footbridge widths at 34 of 43 stations; Western Railway's own platform-indicator abbreviations and the exact wording of announcements; **any recording of train sounds**; official kilometre slabs for fares and the post-July rule for higher-class travel; the **2026 timetable** line by line (the m-Indicator database is the route, via a GitHub Action); the first CSMT train of the day; Central's peak headway and terminus turnaround times; the current state of the Kurla elevated Harbour station, the CSMT Harbour plans, the Parel rebuild, the Elphinstone double-decker and Western Railway's gate project; whether the Centre approved the station renamings; depths of every Line 3 station except Terminal 2; Monorail platform and beam heights; and whole-train base liveries. Above all, nearly every web fact here came from search-result extracts rather than full pages, so numbers shown to players (fares, fines, timings) deserve a spot check once the sites are reachable, while the geometry, measured directly from OpenStreetMap, can be used as it stands.

## Conclusion

The striking result is how little of Mumbai's railway the game has to invent. Station positions, platform outlines and numbers, the tracks along each edge and the footbridge spans are already in the project's own data at 1:1 scale, and the real timetable explains the rhythm the simulation needs; what is missing is mostly the third dimension (heights of platforms, decks and roofs) and the sensory layer (sounds, voices, exact liveries). The work ahead is therefore less research than modelling: a 0.9 m platform, a deck at about 7 m, a roof, and a dozen hand-built buildings where history makes the difference (Chhatrapati Chai Terminus, Byculla, Reay Road, Mumbai Central, Churchgate and the two-tier Sandhurst Road).

The less obvious lesson is that the locals and the metro are two opposite games sharing one city. The locals are open, cheap, gate-less and run on trust and random checks, with crowds so dense that the train is a social space of door groups, bhajans and fourth seats; the metro is gated, timed and quiet, and a Line 3 monthly pass costs more than twice an AC local pass. Because the game clock runs far faster than its trains, neither can sell rush hour through frequency; both must sell it through people, which the load table, the station weights and the culture above now make possible with real numbers behind them.
