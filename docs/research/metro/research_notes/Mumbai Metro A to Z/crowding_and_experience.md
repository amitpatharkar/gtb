## 1. Daily and hourly ridership: totals per line, hourly profile, weekday vs weekend, festivals, monsoon

### Takeaway
In late 2026 the metro carries roughly 1–1.2 million journeys on a weekday: Line 1 about 5.5 lakh (550,000), the MMRDA lines
(2A/7/2B/9) about 3–3.7 lakh, and Line 3 about 2.25 lakh on weekdays (1.3 lakh on weekends). Records are set on heavy-rain
days in June–July, and festivals (Ganeshotsav, New Year's Eve) get late-night or all-night service; no official hourly
profile was found, but peak headways, peak-only short-loop trains and Ghatkopar's peak share (~120k of ~175k daily
movements in the two peaks) show sharp morning and evening peaks.

### Inferences
- Weekday estimate for November 2026: Line 1 ~5.5 lakh + MMMOCL lines ~3.0–3.5 lakh on an ordinary day (3.73 lakh record) +
  Line 3 ~2.25–2.5 lakh (trend plus the 2 Nov frequency boost) ≈ 11–12 lakh journeys/day. The "16 lakh" network figure looks
  too high; Wikipedia's 10.57 lakh fits a 2025–26 average.
- Weekend factor: Line 3's September 2026 numbers give weekend ≈ 0.58 × weekday (131,138 / 224,602). Use ~0.55–0.65 for
  commuter lines on Saturday/Sunday in the game (Saturday probably higher than Sunday; not sourced).
- Peak share: at Ghatkopar ~120,000 of ~175,000 daily movements (~69 %) fall in the morning and evening peaks. A believable
  game curve: very low 5:30–7 am, steep rise 8–11 am (top 9–10:30), lunchtime trough at ~35–45 % of peak, second peak
  ~6–8:30 pm (the operator's short-loop trains run exactly 6:23–8:23 pm), tailing off after 10 pm, closed roughly midnight–5:30
  am except festival nights. This shape is an inference from headways and the short-loop window, not an official hourly table.
- Rain days: ridership on the MMRDA lines rose ~25–30 % above the 2024 record level during the 2026 monsoon; for the game, a
  heavy-rain flag could multiply metro crowds by ~1.1–1.3 and shrink road traffic.
- Festival nights: Ganeshotsav evenings and New Year's Eve are good "crowd events" (late trains, families, idols on Line 3,
  all-night Line 3 on 31 December).

## 2. Peak direction, busiest stations and sections, load and crush capacity

### Takeaway
On Line 1 the Andheri–Ghatkopar section carries the heaviest load (hence evening short-loop trains there since 10 August 2026);
a four-coach train holds about 1,750 people and peak-hour capacity (~31,500–35,000 per hour per direction) is about what the
2031 forecast demand of 30,491 needs, so peaks run near crush. On Line 3 the busiest stations are Marol Naka (Line 1
interchange), CSMT, BKC, Siddhivinayak and Santacruz. Official peak-direction splits were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Line 1: the Andheri–Ghatkopar section "carries the highest passenger load"; from 10 August 2026 a mixed-loop pattern runs
  short Ghatkopar–Andheri trains alternating with full Ghatkopar–Versova trains between 6:23 pm and ~8:23/8:24 pm, cutting the
  Andheri–Ghatkopar headway from 3 min 20 s to 3 min, adding over 20 % capacity (~28,000 more trips) and helping over 85 % of
  riders; waits between Versova and Azad Nagar rise to just over 6 minutes in that window, and some riders beyond Andheri
  complained — [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/news-brief/mumbai-metro-line-1-to-introduce-3-minute-peak-hour-headway-from-10-august-add-28000-daily-passenger-capacity); [FPJ](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-one-introduces-short-loop-services-on-andheri-ghatkopar-route-to-ease-peak-hour-crowding); [Curly Tales](https://curlytales.com/india/trending/what-is-mumbai-metros-mixed-loop-plan-for-ghatkopar-andheri-section-why-are-commuters-unhappy/)
- A similar short-loop shuttle was withdrawn in June 2025 after about three months because it "did not achieve its intended
  objectives" — [Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/transport/shuttle-service-on-andheri-ghatkopar-metro-withdrawn-after-three-months-88904)
- Line 1 runs 16 four-car rakes; each four-coach train carries about 1,750 passengers; at peak 36 trips at an average 3 min 20 s
  frequency; MMOPL has proposed six-coach trains (needs lenders' approval through NARCL/IDRCL because of ~₹1,700 crore debt to six
  banks); the Line 3 link at Marol is expected to add further footfall — [FPJ](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-1-may-soon-get-six-coach-trains-to-ease-rush-hour-crowds); [Oneindia](https://www.oneindia.com/mumbai/mumbai-metro-1-line-expansion-to-ease-congestion-011-7906329.html); [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-metro-one-to-get-six-coach-trains-mmopl-proposes-upgrade-to-ease-overcrowding-a525/); [Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/transport/overcrowded-mumbai-metro-1-line-set-for-upgrade-with-six-coach-trains-90638)
- A Gujarati-language report frames the six-coach plan as freedom from "dhakka-mukki" (pushing and shoving) and queues, with
  capacity up "over 50 %" — [News Continuous](https://www.newscontinuous.com/city/mumbai/mumbai-metro-1-to-get-6-coaches-passenger-capacity-to-surge-by-over-50-percent-after-seven-year-wait/)
- Line 1 forecast: PHPDT 30,491 by 2031 — [TCCA](https://tcca.info/consort-digital-to-upgrade-mumbai-metro-line-1-with-mcx-one/)
- Line 3 busiest stations, October–December 2025 (entries + exits): Marol Naka 16.45 lakh; CSMT 10.28 lakh; BKC 7.3 lakh;
  Siddhivinayak and Santacruz ~6.66 lakh each; interchange stations with suburban rail and other metro lines are the main hubs
  — [Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/civic/mumbai-metro-3-records-over-46-lakh-passenger-footfalls-in-december-know-3-most-crowded-stations-91398); [FPJ](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-3-ridership-more-than-doubles-after-aqua-line-opens-till-cuffe-parade-daily-footfall-hits-new-highs)
- Line 3 has dedicated stations at airport Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 (T2 station planned with the airport operator MIAL) —
  [Wikipedia: T2 metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatrapati_Shivaji_Maharaj_International_Airport_-_T2_metro_station); [Wikipedia: Sahar Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahar_Road_metro_station)
- Line 2A station example: Andheri West ~18,500 daily (2023; dated) — [Wikipedia: Andheri West metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andheri_West_metro_station)

### Inferences
- Line 1 peak capacity: 3,600 s / 200 s = 18 trains/h × 1,750 = ~31,500 passengers per hour per direction; with the 3-minute
  short loop, 20 × 1,750 = ~35,000 on Andheri–Ghatkopar. Forecast demand (30,491) is ~87–97 % of that, so in the game peak
  Line 1 coaches should look full-to-crush on Andheri–Ghatkopar and noticeably lighter west of Andheri (Versova–Azad Nagar).
- Per-coach crush on Line 1: 1,750 / 4 ≈ 440 people per coach at full load. For drawing, a peak coach can be packed shoulder to
  shoulder with every seat taken; off-peak ~30–60 per coach (inference; no off-peak load data).
- Line 1 likely morning peak direction: Ghatkopar → Andheri/Chakala/Marol (Central Railway and eastern suburbs commuters heading
  to Andheri East offices, MIDC/SEEPZ and the Western Railway at Andheri); reverse in the evening (supported indirectly by the
  heaviest-section finding and the evening loops starting at Ghatkopar–Andheri; direction itself is not officially stated).
- Line 3: office demand points (BKC, Worli/Acharya Atre Chowk, CSMT/Fort, Cuffe Parade) suggest morning flow southwards and
  towards BKC from Aarey/Marol/Santacruz, reverse in the evening; airport stations add luggage riders all day. Marol Naka's top
  ranking shows the Line 1 ↔ Line 3 transfer is the line's biggest pinch point.
- Lines 2A/7: morning flow towards Andheri (DN Nagar/Andheri West for 2A, Gundavali/WEH for 7) to connect with Line 1 and the
  Western Railway, evening reverse — plausible from geography but not sourced.
- Line 3 at 4 min 25 s peak headway = ~13–14 trains/h; with ~2.25 lakh weekday riders it is far from crush — a "roomy" metro
  in the game compared with Line 1.

### Gaps
- No measured peak-direction splits, section loads or load factors for any line (only Line 1's 2031 PHPDT forecast).
- No official passengers-per-square-metre crush standard or train capacity for Lines 2A/7/3 found in this pass (Line 3 trains
  are widely described as 8-car and 2A/7 as 6-car, but that was not confirmed by a source here).
- No ranking of busiest stations on Lines 1, 2A or 7 for 2025–26.

## 3. Station crowding and crowd control at interchanges

### Takeaway
Ghatkopar (Line 1 ↔ Central Railway) is the classic crush point: up to 175,000 people a day, narrow footbridge and staircases,
exits that pass one person at a time, and auto-rickshaws blocking the road outside. Marol Naka (Line 1 ↔ Line 3) became Line 3's
busiest station after full opening. Gundavali–WEH (Line 7 ↔ Line 1) is a footbridge transfer that needs ~5 extra minutes.
Measures found: extra fare gates, short-loop trains, more trips, standby rakes on rain days.

### Cited Findings
- Ghatkopar: up to 175,000 passengers/day, ~120,000 movements in the peaks — [Indian Infrastructure, 2026 (via search summary)](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2026/03/06/digital-ticketing-building-a-future-ready-afc-system-for-mumbai-metro-line-1/)
- Ghatkopar (blogger, ~2025, anecdotal): crowds spill onto a footbridge and a "tiny staircase"; exits handle one person at a
  time; the terminus should have had more queue space since it serves the metro and a major railway station — [The Idea-Smithy blog](https://ramyapandyan.com/a-noobs-guide-to-the-mumbai-metro/)
- Ghatkopar has 7 sets of automatic fare collection gates; three more were reportedly announced in 2014 (AI-written source, low
  reliability) — [Wikipedia: Ghatkopar metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghatkopar_metro_station); [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Ghatkopar_metro_station)
- Ghatkopar street level (about 2019): autos blocking roads outside; police promised to check auto stands and keep autos off the
  road — [DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/mumbai/report-unplanned-development-spells-chaos-at-ghatkopar-station-2736462)
- A July overhead-equipment fault on Line 1 caused delays and a "stampede-like rush" on platforms (year not given; low-reliability
  source) — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Ghatkopar_metro_station)
- Marol Naka was Line 3's busiest station in Oct–Dec 2025 (16.45 lakh entries + exits) — [Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/civic/mumbai-metro-3-records-over-46-lakh-passenger-footfalls-in-december-know-3-most-crowded-stations-91398)

## 4. Passenger experience: tickets, security, women's coach, announcements, rules, network, commuter types

## 5. Comparison with the local trains; change after Line 3 opened fully

### Takeaway
The metro (~1.1 million journeys a day) is a fraction of the suburban railway (6.5–7.5 million) and far less crowded, but costs
more (a viral comparison: ₹3,500/month on Line 3 vs ₹1,335 for an AC local pass). Line 3 ridership roughly doubled after the full
Cuffe Parade opening (Oct 2025) and has kept growing to ~2.25 lakh per weekday, yet remains far below its 1.5 million forecast.

### Cited Findings
- Local trains: around 6.5–7 million commuters daily on Western and Central Railways (Hindustan Times, 2024, as quoted in the
  summary); a tourist guide says 7.5 million and that trains built for 1,750 routinely carry 4,500 (unclear sourcing) —
  [Mumbai Itinerary transport guide](https://mumbaiitinerary.com/mumbai-transport-guide); [Wikipedia: Mumbai Suburban Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Suburban_Railway)
- Fares: local ₹5–15 (second class) vs metro ₹20–30 for similar distances (secondary guide) — [Mumbai Metro Route fares guide 2026](https://mumbaimetroroute.com/guides/mumbai-metro-fares-guide)
- Viral fare comparison (Oct 2025): Andheri–Churchgate AC local pass ~₹1,335/month vs ~₹3,500/month on Line 3; netizens: "pay
  extra for peaceful travel" — [FPJ](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/pay-extra-for-peaceful-travel-netizens-react-as-commuter-compares-rates-between-ac-local-train-vs-mumbai-metro-3); [Curly Tales](https://curlytales.com/india/trending/mumbai-metro-for-vs-local-train-for-commuters-fare-comparison-goes-viral-netizens-share-opinions/)
- Line 3 after full opening: monthly ridership from 19.7 lakh (Sept 2025) to 38.63 lakh (Oct 2025) and 46.56 lakh (Dec 2025);
  weekday average from 75,052 to 224,602 (Sept 2026) — [FPJ](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-3-ridership-more-than-doubles-after-aqua-line-opens-till-cuffe-parade-daily-footfall-hits-new-highs); [FPJ one-year](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-metro-3-aqua-line-crosses-72-crore-passenger-journeys-in-one-year-mmrc-announces-time-capsule-stamp-trail)
