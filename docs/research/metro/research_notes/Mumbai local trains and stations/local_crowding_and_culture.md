## 1. Ridership: daily passengers per line, trend since the pandemic, season tickets, AC locals

### Takeaway
The whole suburban network carries roughly 6–7.5 million (60–75 lakh) passengers on an ordinary day in 2025–26 (Central Railway, including Harbour and Trans-Harbour, about 40–45 lakh; Western Railway about 28–31 lakh), still below pre-pandemic levels on Western Railway because of work from home and the new Metro lines; record days reach far higher (5 January 2026: Central 72.18 lakh, Western 52.07 lakh). Roughly two-thirds or more of riders travel on season tickets (passes); Western Railway's AC locals carry about 1.5–1.6 lakh a day and are growing fast.

### Inferences
- The 34.87 → 29.52 lakh figure fits Western Railway alone (WR's quarter total of 25.60 crore ÷ 91 days ≈ 28 lakh a day), so a fair 2025–26 split is **WR ≈ 28–31 lakh/day, CR (Main + Harbour + Trans-Harbour) ≈ 40–45 lakh/day, total ≈ 70–75 lakh on a normal weekday** (CR's 129 million in January ÷ 31 days ≈ 41.6 lakh/day, including weekends). The record day (≈1.24 crore) is about 1.7× a normal weekday — a good "special day" multiplier for the game.
- The user's guesses (Western ~3.5 million, Central ~4 million) are close: Western ≈ 3.0 million now (3.5 million was pre-pandemic), Central ≈ 4.0–4.5 million.
- AC locals carry only about 5 % of Western Railway's riders on about 8 % of services, so in the game AC rakes should look visibly less packed than ordinary rakes on the same line, except at the doors in peak hours (see section 3).
- With about two-thirds on passes, most peak riders are daily regulars who know where their coach stops — useful for "regulars stand exactly at the coach mark" behaviour on platforms.

## 2. Hourly and weekly profile, peak direction, crush-load figures

### Takeaway
Peaks run about 7:30/8–11 am towards Churchgate/CSMT and about 5–9 pm (to 10:30 pm on the Virar side) away from town, with trains every 3–5 minutes; in the peak direction a train carries about 2.5–4 times its rated load ("super dense crush load", 14–16 standing persons per square metre). Sundays bring planned "mega blocks" (track work, roughly 10/11 am–3/4 pm) that cancel services on stretches and push crowds onto fewer trains.

### Cited Findings
**Peak windows and direction**
- Rush hours defined as 7:00–11:00 am and 4:00–9:00 pm (older World Bank working paper) — [World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 4395](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/152531468268835999/pdf/wps4395.pdf)
- From the north (Vasai, Virar, Borivali) towards Churchgate the morning peak is 8:00–11:30 am; towards Virar the evening peak is 5:00–10:30 pm (travel guide) — [Mumbai77](https://www.mumbai77.com/city/6209/travel/local-train-travel-things-to-know/)
- 07:30–10:30 heading south or towards employment areas; 17:30–21:00 towards the suburbs; busiest corridors reach 14–16 standing persons per m² (aggregator page) — [Mapa Metro](https://mapa-metro.com/en/India/Mumbai/Mumbai-Suburban-Railway-map.htm)
- Churchgate: peak 8–11 am and 5–9 pm with trains every 3–5 minutes — [Mumbai Metro Route: Churchgate](https://mumbaimetroroute.com/mumbai-local-train/stations/churchgate); 7–11 am and 5–9 pm, every 3–5 minutes — [MumbaiHop](https://mumbaihop.com/mumbai-local-trains-the-only-guide-you-need-to-travel-like-a-pro/)
- Anecdotal: morning 7:00–9:15/10:00, evening 5:15–8:30/9:30 pm ("about 70 % accurate") — [Quora](https://www.quora.com/What-is-the-peak-hour-of-Mumbai-local)
- Peak frequency reaches about 3 minutes on the busiest corridors (not confirmed by an official source) — [Mapa Metro](https://mapa-metro.com/en/India/Mumbai/Mumbai-Suburban-Railway-map.htm)

**Crush load and capacity**
- "Super dense crush load" is the railway's own term for peak trains: roughly 14–16 standing persons per m²; coaches built for about 200 carry over 500 (compare: about 6 per m² is a common practical limit elsewhere) — [Wikipedia: Crush load](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crush_load)
- 12-car rake capacity 3,504 (1,168 seated + 2,336 standing) — [Wikipedia: Mumbai Suburban Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Suburban_Railway)
- 12-car train: about 2,400 is a comfortable load; peak loads run above 6,000 per rake; 16 persons per m² — [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/mumbai-suburban-railway-problem-best-brts-solution/)
- A 9-car rake carried about 4,700 at peak against a rated capacity of about 1,710 (≈ 2.7×), the reason 9-car rakes were replaced by 12-car ones (Mumbai Urban Transport Project document, 2000s) — [MUTP document (CORE)](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/9309891.pdf)
- "Normal capacity of each train is 1,700; during peak hours more than 5,000 people crowd" (2011, 9-car era) — [Vikalpa, 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0256090920110105)
- A ridership survey reports peak loads up to 7,463 commuters per train, over 200 % above designed capacity — [Academia.edu survey](https://www.academia.edu/95353066/Survey_of_Passenger_Ridership_in_Mumbai_Suburban_Railway)
- About 1,100 quoted for a 12-car train (apparently seated only) — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/maharashtra/life-on-the-lines-mounting-risks-of-travel-in-mumbai-3586351)
- Older Central Railway 12-car seat split: about 1,161 seats — about 221 second-class and 24 first-class seats for women, about 678 second-class and 164 first-class for men, plus small blocks for senior citizens, disabled passengers and luggage (article about 12 years old) — [DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/mumbai/report-need-for-more-ladies-coaches-but-officials-undecided-2003568)

**Sundays, mega blocks, late night**
- Sunday 15 November 2026: "jumbo block" on Central, Harbour and Trans-Harbour lines; Panvel–Vashi both tracks affected — [eSakal](https://esakal.com/mumbai/sunday-15th-november-mumbai-local-train-jumbo-block-impact-on-central-harbour-and-trans-harbour-services-mvk02)
- Typical Harbour block: Panvel–Vashi services suspended about 11:05 am–4:05 pm, several Harbour and Trans-Harbour services cancelled; in another block, up services Panvel→Thane between 11:02 am and 3:53 pm and down services Thane→Panvel 10:01 am–3:20 pm cancelled; some blocks run Panvel–Kurla specials instead — [Free Press Journal (Chhath Puja block)](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-local-train-update-central-railway-announces-mega-block-on-thane-kalyan-panvel-vashi-routes-on-chhath-puja-check-timings-mumbai-news); [Free Press Journal (18 Oct block)](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-local-train-update-central-harbour-lines-to-face-mega-block-on-sunday-october-18-western-railway-services-unaffected)
- Western Railway often skips the Sunday block ("relief to commuters"); Central even ran a block on Diwali Sunday — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-local-train-update-central-railway-announces-mega-block-on-diwali-sunday-western-railway-offers-relief-to-commuters-mumbai-news); but blocks on all three lines happened on Sunday 25 October 2026 — [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-local-train-mega-block-on-october-25-2026-services-on-central-harbour-and-western-lines-to-be-disrupted-on-sunday-check-details-a517/amp/)
- Photo essay on the first local at 3:25 am, "a gentler ride" (title only in extract) — [Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/696805/photo-essay-on-mumbais-first-local-train-at-3-25-am-a-gentler-ride)

### Inferences
- The user's "4,500–5,000 per train vs capacity ~1,700" is the **9-car era** figure (rated ~1,710). For today's 12-car rakes: rated ~3,500 (seated ~1,170), comfortable ~2,400, peak 5,000–6,000+, worst cases ~7,400. Per coach (12 cars): seated ~95–100, rated ~290, peak ~450–550.

## 3. Busiest stations; fast vs slow; first vs second class; ladies coaches; AC locals and ticketless riders

### Takeaway
Thane and Kalyan are the busiest stations by originating passengers (FY2023-24: about 93 million and 84 million a year), with CSMT, Andheri, Dadar, Dombivli, Borivali and Churchgate in the next group; news reports put Thane's total footfall at 5–8 lakh a day. Fast trains from Virar and Kalyan side are the most packed; ladies coaches see fights over space; AC locals are less crowded overall but face ticketless riders and door blocking in peak hours.

### Inferences
- Station "crowd weight" for the game (relative peak platform crowd, derived): Thane, Kalyan, Dadar, Andheri, CSMT, Kurla, Ghatkopar, Borivali, Churchgate, Bandra = highest; Dombivli, Diva, Virar, Vasai, Nalasopara, Bhayandar, Mira Road = very high at the doors (origin of door groups); small South Mumbai stations (Marine Lines, Charni Road, Grant Road, Sandhurst Road, Masjid, Reay Road, Cotton Green) = moderate, but heavy alighting in the morning and boarding in the evening.
- Within the game map (Colaba to Bandra/Andheri/Kurla/Powai), the big origin stations (Virar, Borivali, Thane, Kalyan) are off-map, so trains should enter the map already full in the morning (inbound) and leave full in the evening (outbound). Dadar, Kurla, Bandra and Andheri are interchange scrums in both directions.
- Coach-level variation: first class ≈ 60–80 % of second-class density (it is smaller but cheaper per space); ladies coaches crowded but with fewer door-hangers; luggage compartment (one end) holds dabbawalas' crates, fish baskets and vendors; AC coaches crowded mainly at the doors.

## 4. Boarding and riding culture

### Takeaway
Riding culture is built around regulars: door groups who hold the doorway, the unofficial "fourth seat" on three-seat benches, about 200 bhajan (devotional singing) groups, card games on newspaper-covered laps, dabbawalas' tiffin crates and Koli fisherwomen's baskets in the luggage compartment, and hawkers, beggars and transgender persons asking for money despite repeated police drives. Monsoon floods stop the Harbour and Main lines at Sion–Kurla; festivals add special night trains.

### Cited Findings
**Seats, doors and daily rituals**

## 5. Safety facts (deaths, falls, track crossing, police)

### Takeaway
About 2,300 people died on Mumbai's suburban rail network in 2025 (≈ 6 a day), down 7 % from 2024; about half died crossing tracks and about 525 fell from moving trains; Thane, Kalyan and Borivali sections are worst. After four to five commuters fell from two crowded trains near Mumbra on 9 June 2025, the Railway Board decided all new Mumbai suburban trains get automatic doors and existing ones will be retrofitted.

### Cited Findings
- Government Railway Police (GRP) data: 2,287 deaths in 2025, down 7 % from 2,468 in 2024; injuries 2,554 (from 2,697); 1,063 died crossing tracks (about half; Thane section 144); 525 died falling from moving trains (Kalyan section highest, 98); deaths by section: Thane 278, Kalyan 266, Borivali 244 — [Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/civic/mumbai-local-train-fatality-falls-7-in-2025-track-crossing-and-overcrowding-main-reasons-for-death-91328); [Railway Supply](https://www.railway.supply/mumbai-railway-commuter-deaths-in-2025-numbers-fall-risks-persist/)
- 2024: 570 deaths from falling off local trains (different source; may not be comparable) — [Railway Supply](https://www.railway.supply/mumbai-railway-commuter-deaths-in-2025-numbers-fall-risks-persist/)
- January–May 2025 (RTI reply): 922 accidental deaths, 210 from falls from overcrowded trains — [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/states/west/maharashtra/mumbai-rail-network-deaths-in-five-months-rti-application-195708)
- 29,970 deaths and 30,214 injuries on the suburban network over 11 years (GRP data) — [Zee News](https://zeenews.india.com/india/lifeline-or-death-trap-nearly-30000-dead-in-11-years-in-packed-mumbai-locals-2914043.html)

## 6. Station exit crowds: foot-overbridges, skywalks, taxi and auto queues

### Takeaway
Foot-overbridges (FOBs) are the choke point: the Elphinstone Road–Parel FOB crush on 29 September 2017 killed 23 in the morning rush during rain, and the Army then built three new footbridges; Thane still sees near-crushes when an escalator fails at peak. Skywalks and taxi/auto queues are known features but were not quantified in sources found.

### Cited Findings
- 29 September 2017: crush on the narrow footbridge linking Elphinstone Road and Parel stations during the morning rush; commuters sheltering from heavy rain, several trains arriving together; at least 23 dead and over 30 injured; reports differ on the trigger — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2017/9/29/india-at-least-22-killed-in-mumbai-station-stampede); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ani/mumbai-s-elphinstone-stampede-at-least-22-dead-30-injured-117092900512_1.html); [Governance Now](https://www.governancenow.com/news/regular-story/22-die-in-stampede-on-narrow-mumbai-foot-over-bridge)
- Regular commuters said the FOBs were too narrow for the numbers; in February 2018 the Indian Army opened new Bailey-bridge footbridges at Elphinstone Road, Currey Road and Ambivli — [Wikipedia: 2017 Mumbai stampede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Mumbai_stampede)
- Thane: an escalator breakdown during peak rush triggered a near-stampede on the FOB — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/thane-railway-station-escalator-breakdown-triggers-near-stampede-during-peak-rush-commuters-face-severe-hardship-on-fob)
- Mumbai Skywalk Project exists (elevated walkways from stations; no details extracted) — [Wikipedia: Mumbai Skywalk Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Skywalk_Project)

### Inferences
- Game: when several trains arrive together at a peak hour, FOB stairs should become the slowest part of the station (people queue on the stairs, flow one way); outside, a crowd spills onto the station road with taxi and auto queues (autos only north of Mahim/Sion per the game's rules).
- Mention Elphinstone only as a safety fact (e.g. why new wide FOBs exist), never as a scene.

### Gaps
- No numbers found for FOB widths, exit flows per minute, skywalk usage, or taxi/auto queue lengths at Dadar, Andheri, Bandra, Kurla or CSMT.
- Elphinstone Road station's renaming (to Prabhadevi) was not confirmed by a source in this session.
