## 1. Local trains: ticket types and fare tables (second class, first class, AC; season, tourist, platform; concessions; 2025–2026 revisions)

### Takeaway
Suburban fares have not changed since the May 2022 AC/first-class cut. The July 2025 and 26 Dec 2025 national fare rises left
suburban tickets and season tickets alone. Second class starts at ₹5 and tops out around ₹20 for about 60 km (roughly 30 paise
per km, in ₹5 steps). First class starts at about ₹25. AC starts at ₹30 base for 5 km, ₹35 as charged. Season tickets come in
monthly, quarterly (2.7× monthly), half-yearly (5.4×) and yearly (10.8×). Tourist tickets give unlimited travel for 1, 3 or 5
days. A platform ticket is ₹10 for 2 hours, raised to ₹50 for a few days during crowd surges. No official, current km-slab table
could be opened. The examples below come from station fare charts and news reports.

**Children, students, senior citizens**

### Inferences
- The AC charged minimum of ₹35 against the ₹30 "base" probably comes from adding GST to the base and rounding up to the next ₹5.
  That rounding would explain why both figures appear. This is unverified.
- A workable game fare model, consistent with every reliable data point: second class ₹5 up to about 10 km, then +₹5 per band up
  to ₹20 at about 60 km and ₹25–30 beyond. First class about 10× second class with a ₹25 minimum (Churchgate–Virar ₹100). AC
  about 1.1–1.2× first class with a ₹35 minimum (Churchgate–Virar about ₹105–115). Monthly pass about 15–20 second-class
  singles; quarterly 2.7×, half-yearly 5.4×, yearly 10.8× monthly.
- Fares unchanged since 2022 make a good RJ joke ("the only thing in Mumbai that hasn't gone up").

### Gaps
- The official distance-slab tables (km boundaries for second, first and AC) could not be opened; the WR and CR fare pages are blocked.
- Current first-class single fares, beyond Churchgate–Virar ₹100 and the ₹25 minimum, were not found.
- Whether the first-class single cut in 2022 was also 50 % is unclear.
- Whether the ₹5.90 UPI charge on passes above ₹2,000 is real could not be checked (headline only).
- No Mumbai-specific senior-citizen rule was found. Suburban senior concessions are not mentioned in any 2025–26 source.
- How child season tickets are priced is not stated.

## 2. Local trains: validity and route rules

### Takeaway
A journey ticket (counter, ATVM print-out or app) must be used to start the journey within 1 hour of issue. A return ticket's
return half lasts until midnight of the following day. A paperless ticket cannot be cancelled. A ticket that cannot be shown on
the phone counts as no ticket. Season tickets are for a fixed origin–destination pair and keep their expiry when upgraded. No
source spelled out the "via" rules (via Dadar, Kurla, Wadala, Thane and so on) for Mumbai routes.

### Cited Findings
- Official UTS FAQ: "The journey should commence within one hour after booking paperless ticket", and within one hour after
  printing a paper ticket from an ATVM or Co-TVM. "If the passenger is not able to show the ticket to the ticket checking staff, it
  should be considered ticketless travel." — [UTS on Mobile FAQ](https://www.utsonmobile.indianrail.gov.in/RDS/policy/faq_); [UTS Android help PDF](https://utsonmobile.indianrail.gov.in/images/HELP_ANDROID_Paperless.pdf)
- Counter and machine journey tickets carry a date-time stamp, and travel must start within 1 hour of it. — [Locals of Mumbai](https://www.localsofmumbai.com/mumbai-suburban-railway/); [MetroEasy](https://metroeasy.com/india/mumbai-suburban-railway/); [Maps of India](https://www.mapsofindia.com/maps/mumbai/local-train-network.html)
- Cancellation: paperless tickets cannot be cancelled. A paper ticket printed from the app booking can be cancelled only at a UTS
  counter within 1 hour of printing, with no cash refund. — [UTS on Mobile FAQ](https://www.utsonmobile.indianrail.gov.in/RDS/policy/faq_)
- Return ticket (Western Railway's own page, as summarised in the search extract): valid until midnight of the next day; a Friday purchase is valid until Monday. — [Western Railway, Ticket Booking Office](https://wr.indianrailways.gov.in/view_section.jsp?lang=0&id=0%2C6%2C630)
- The UTS one-hour rule "may change time to time" (official help text). — [UTS on Mobile FAQ](https://www.utsonmobile.indianrail.gov.in/RDS/policy/faq_)
- Users of the UTS app must keep the mobile ticket available to show on the phone screen, or they are treated as ticketless (headline). — [FPJ](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-users-of-uts-app-should-keep-mobile-tickets-on-phone-screen-or-they-would-be-considered-travelling-ticketless)
- Existing UTS-issued season passes stay valid until expiry after UTS shut down; renewals go through RailOne. — [LatestLY](https://www.latestly.com/india/information/uts-app-shutdown-what-will-happen-to-your-existing-season-passes-and-r-wallet-balance-7260935.html)
- Upgraded first-class passes (AC upgrade) keep the original expiry date (see §1). — [Inshorts](https://inshorts.com/en/news/firstclass-passengers-in-mumbai-can-upgrade-passes-to-travel-in-ac-locals-cr-1662458166310)
- Tourist tickets are valid until midnight of the last day; platform tickets for 2 hours (see §1).

### Inferences
- Game rule set: a single ticket is "live" for 60 game minutes from purchase to start the ride (to boarding, not to arrival); it
  covers one ride from A to B in the bought class. A return ticket adds a second ride that can start any time until the following
  midnight. A pass covers unlimited rides between its two stations, and stations in between, until expiry.
- Mumbai tickets between lines are probably priced by the shortest route with a printed "via" (for example, Western-to-Central
  "via Dadar"). This is unsourced, so the game could simply charge by the shortest track distance.

### Gaps
- Official "via" or route rules, and whether a ticket allows breaking the journey, were not found.
- Whether the 1-hour rule applies to counter tickets officially (only third-party sources say so) is unconfirmed. The WR page text could not be opened.
- The exact wording of the WR return-ticket rule (weekday vs weekend) comes from a search summary and should be checked.

## 3. Local trains: where and how tickets are bought (counters, ATVMs, sahayaks, UTS → RailOne, geo-fencing, station QR codes, Mumbai One, gates)

### Takeaway
In November 2026 a Mumbai commuter buys from:
- a booking-office window (cash or UPI);
- an ATVM (automatic ticket vending machine) by smart card or UPI QR; Western Railway is adding 325 digital ATVMs to 501;
- "M-UTS Sahayaks", staff with phones who sell app tickets in the queue;
- the RailOne app, which replaced the UTS app on 1 March 2026. Paperless tickets are booked from anywhere except inside the
  station or near the tracks; the outer geo-fence was removed in April–May 2024;
- the state's Mumbai One app (single-journey QR only, no suburban passes or returns).

There are still no fare gates at local stations. Western Railway planned metro-style gates at some stations in 2025; their
status is unknown.

## 4. Local trains: ticket checking, penalties, the ticketless problem and fraud

### Takeaway
There are no gates, so enforcement is by ticket-checking staff (TCs and TTEs, plus Sectional Commercial Ticket Inspectors). They
check at station exits, on trains and in planned drives: "station checks, ambush checks, fortress checks, intensive
inspections, mega drives". They verify app QR codes on handheld terminals (HHTs), and Western Railway now gives some of them body cameras.

From 1 July 2026 (some reports: 19–20 June) the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2026 doubled the minimum penalty for
ticketless or irregular travel from ₹250 to ₹500, plus the fare. Section 138 became a civil penalty, so court only follows if
you don't pay. A man in a women's coach now pays ₹2,500. Penalties rise 10 % every three years.

Enforcement in 2026 is heavy, especially in AC locals:
- Central Railway's Mumbai Division: 4.30 lakh cases and ₹30.13 crore in April–May 2026;
- one day (18 Apr 2026): 9,509 caught and ₹71.38 lakh;
- AC locals: Western Railway catches about 330 ticketless riders a day.

Forged app tickets (edited screenshots, fake apps, AI-made passes) lead to FIRs and police custody.

- Rough odds: Central Railway's Mumbai division catches roughly 1.3–2.3 lakh cases a month. Against several million daily rides
  (crowding notes), the real chance of being checked on one ride is low, well under 1 %. A game should raise it for fun: say
  5–10 % per ride, much higher in AC and first class, and a "mega drive" event where a checker line blocks the exit.
- Game flow when caught: the checker asks for the ticket, scans the phone QR (green or red), and states the penalty (₹500 + fare;

  goes straight to the police.

## 5. Metro: tickets, gates, time limits, overstay and penalties, passes and discounts, security (additions to the existing notes)

### Takeaway
Each operator sets its own rules:
- Line 1 (Metro One): exit within 20 minutes if you leave at the station where you entered; 75 minutes is the reported limit for
  other stations. Overstay costs ₹10 per hour, capped at ₹50. Tickets are valid only for the day of issue. Paper QR tickets
  replaced plastic tokens from Jan 2020; in 2026 there are about 1.9 lakh paper single-journey transactions and 65,000 NCMC
  card users a day.
- Lines 2A/7 (MMMOCL): a QR ticket is good for one trip and expires at the end of service that day. A paper single-journey
  ticket must be used within 60 minutes of issue. Over-travel is paid at a customer-care desk at the exit. The exact time limits
  and penalties are on each station's penalty board.
- Line 3 (MMRC): NCMC-based tourist passes (₹280 one day, ₹700 three days) and a 25 % student concession (school, then
  college) since 2026. Security is baggage X-ray plus frisking, but an FPJ sting got firecrackers through.

### Inferences
- Game metro flow:
  1. Security: bag on the X-ray belt and a walk through the door-frame detector, with a guard's hand-held wand. A separate
     curtained frisking booth for women is standard in India, but no Mumbai source confirms it.
  2. Ticket: QR on the phone, a paper QR from the counter or machine, or a card.
  3. Tap or scan at the flap gate to enter.
  4. Ride, then tap or scan out. The gate computes the fare.
  - Exiting where you entered after more than 20 minutes, or staying in the paid area too long, costs ₹10 per hour up to ₹50
    (Line 1 rule; reuse it for every line for simplicity).
  - Over-travel takes you to the customer-care desk to pay the difference.
  - Tickets expire at the end of service.
- Line 3's underground "no signal" problem (crowding notes) plus "book before you go down" plus the NCMC-only tourist pass makes a
  natural puzzle: the player must carry a card or a pre-bought QR before entering.

## 6. What tickets and screens look like (layout fields); platform-ticket handling

### Takeaway
No source in reach showed or described the field layout of a Mumbai suburban paper ticket, a UTS or RailOne app ticket screen,
or a metro QR ticket. Search results returned generic or foreign ticket designs. What is known: a ticket carries a date-time
stamp, and validity is counted from it; app tickets carry a QR or barcode that a checker's handheld terminal verifies; platform
tickets are station-specific and time-limited. Layout work for the game should use photos the owner takes, or clearly
fictional designs.

### Cited Findings
- Journey tickets carry a date and time stamp; validity (1 hour to start) runs from it. — [Locals of Mumbai](https://www.localsofmumbai.com/mumbai-suburban-railway/)
- App tickets show a QR or barcode that ticket examiners scan with a handheld terminal (HHT); a fake shows as invalid when scanned. — [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/mumbai/mumbai-local-train-two-arrested-for-travelling-in-first-class-with-fake-uts-passes-a522)
- Station QR codes for booking existed and were being made dynamic (time-varying) to stop fraud. — [Knocksense](https://www.knocksense.com/amp/story/mumbai/dynamic-qr-codes-coming-to-mumbai-locals-to-stop-ticket-fraud)
- Generic point-of-sale ticket contents: date and time of issue, value, fare product, passenger class (adult or child), unique
  ticket number, issuing machine number. This is a general toolkit, not Mumbai-specific. — [SSATP Fares Toolkit](https://www.ssatp.org/sites/ssatp/files/publications/Toolkits/Fares%20Toolkit%20content/fare-collection-technologies/tickets/printed-at-point-of-issue.html)
- Mumbai One gives a dynamic QR, and metro QR tickets are scanned at the gate (see §3 and §5). — [Musafir](https://in.musafir.com/blog/mumbai-one-app)
- Platform ticket: ₹10, 2 hours, the issuing station only, one person, no boarding (see §1). — [RailMitra](https://www.railmitra.com/blog/railway-platform-ticket-rules)

### Inferences
- A safe game ticket template using only fields evidenced in general (station names are real places, so they are fine):

  ```
  JOURNEY | 2nd/1st/AC
  FROM → TO (VIA …)
  ADULT n / CHILD n
  ₹ fare
  date, time
  ticket no.
  "Start journey within 1 hour"
  ```

  The app version shows the same fields above a QR code that changes, with a parody app name. A season ticket shows FROM–TO,
  class, the valid-from and valid-to dates, and the holder's name and ID.
- For the checker scene: green tick or red cross on the checker's handheld after scanning.
